## Goal
Compare fast but drifting **gyroscope integration** against drift-free but vibration-noisy **accelerometer gravity tilt (`atan2`)**, and fuse both in 2 lines of Python using a **complementary IMU filter** ($\hat{\theta}_k = \alpha(\hat{\theta}_{k-1} + \omega_{\text{gyro}}\Delta t) + (1-\alpha)\theta_{\text{acc},k}$).

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Gyroscope integration bias drift versus accelerometer gravity tilt (`atan2`)**: Why integrating a gyroscope ($\theta \leftarrow \theta + \omega \Delta t$) accumulates constant bias $b_g \cdot t$ over time, while an accelerometer measures gravity angle via `atan2(a_y, a_z)` without long-term drift.

## Learn

### Idea 1: Gyroscope integration bias drift versus accelerometer gravity tilt (`atan2`)
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Inertial measurement unit ("Operation" & "Errors")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_measurement_unit) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Inertial measurement unit`**, **`Operation`**, and **`Performance`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip ring-laser submarine navigation.
  - **Key takeaway:** A 6-axis **Inertial Measurement Unit (`IMU`, such as `MPU6050`)** combines a 3-axis **gyroscope** (measuring angular velocity $\omega$ in `rad/s`) and a 3-axis **accelerometer** (measuring linear acceleration plus Earth's $9.81\text{ m/s}^2$ gravity pull). Integrating the gyroscope ($\theta_k = \theta_{k-1} + \omega_{\text{gyro}}\Delta t$) gives smooth fast motion, but even a tiny constant **gyroscope bias** $b_g = +0.05\text{ rad/s}$ drifts by $0.05 \times 10\text{ s} = 0.50\text{ rad}$ ($28.6^\circ$!) in just 10 seconds. Conversely, computing tilt from gravity via $\theta_{\text{acc}} = \operatorname{atan2}(a_y, a_z)$ never drifts over hours, but shakes whenever the robot frame vibrates.
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun — Accelerometer Basics](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/accelerometer-basics) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`What is an Accelerometer?`** and **`How to Select an Accelerometer`**.

### Idea 2: Complementary IMU filter fusion ($\alpha \cdot \text{gyro} + (1 - \alpha) \cdot \theta_{\text{acc}}$)
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Low-pass filter ("Discrete-time realization")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pass_filter) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Low-pass filter`** and **`Discrete-time realization`** (how a recursive weighted average smooths noisy measurements).
  - **What to skip:** Skip Butterworth circuit synthesis.
  - **Key takeaway:** Because the gyroscope is trustworthy at fast timescales and the accelerometer is trustworthy at slow timescales, a **complementary IMU filter** blends them in a single line using blending weight $\alpha \in [0.95, 0.995]$ (whose weights $\alpha$ and $1 - \alpha$ sum to $1.0$):
    `theta_est = alpha * (theta_est + omega_gyro * dt) + (1.0 - alpha) * theta_acc`.
  - **Alternative resource:** [MathsIsFun — Weighted Mean](https://www.mathsisfun.com/data/weighted-mean.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Review how two weights $\alpha$ and $1 - \alpha$ combine two estimates.

### Bridge Notes
On a balancing robot or mobile base equipped with an `MPU6050` IMU, neither sensor works alone: **gyroscope integration** ($\theta_{\text{gyro}} \leftarrow \theta_{\text{gyro}} + \omega_{\text{gyro}}\Delta t$) responds instantly to fast motion but suffers from unbounded **gyroscope bias drift**, while **accelerometer gravity tilt**:

$$
\theta_{\text{acc}} = \operatorname{atan2}(a_y,\ a_z)
$$

never drifts over long timescales because Earth's gravity vector always points straight down, yet shakes violently whenever motor vibrations accelerate the chassis. The **complementary IMU filter** combines the best of both sensors in two lines of code at each sample step $\Delta t$:

$$
\hat{\theta}_k = \alpha\left(\hat{\theta}_{k-1} + \omega_{\text{gyro},k}\Delta t\right) + (1 - \alpha)\theta_{\text{acc},k}, \qquad \tau_c = \frac{\alpha \Delta t}{1 - \alpha}
$$

Here $\tau_c$ is the crossover time constant: for $\Delta t = 0.010\text{ s}$ ($100\text{ Hz}$) and blending weight $\alpha = 0.98$, $\tau_c = \frac{0.98 \times 0.010}{0.02} = 0.490\text{ s}$. Motions faster than $0.49\text{ s}$ come $98\%$ from the clean gyroscope, while over timescales longer than $0.49\text{ s}$ the $2\%$ accelerometer correction anchors the angle to gravity. Even when the gyroscope has a constant offset bias $b_g$, the **complementary IMU filter** completely stops unbounded runaway drift, bounding steady-state bias error to just $\Delta\theta_{\text{ss}} = \tau_c \cdot b_g$.

```python
import math


def accel_tilt_rad(ay_m_s2, az_m_s2):
    return math.atan2(ay_m_s2, az_m_s2)


def run_complementary_imu(steps=1000, dt=0.01, gyro_bias=0.05, alpha=0.98):
    theta_gyro_only = 0.0
    theta_comp = 0.0
    for k in range(steps):
        vib = 0.10 if (k % 2 == 0) else -0.10
        theta_acc = vib
        omega_meas = 0.0 + gyro_bias
        theta_gyro_only += omega_meas * dt
        theta_comp = alpha * (theta_comp + omega_meas * dt) + (1.0 - alpha) * theta_acc
    return theta_gyro_only, theta_comp


tilt_0 = accel_tilt_rad(0.0, 9.81)
tilt_45 = accel_tilt_rad(6.9367, 6.9367)
g_end, c_end = run_complementary_imu()
tau_c = (0.98 * 0.01) / (1.0 - 0.98)

print(f"Accel tilt at (ay=0, az=9.81): {tilt_0:.3f} rad | at (ay=az): {tilt_45:.4f} rad")
print(f"After 10.0 s with +0.05 rad/s gyro bias: Gyro-only={g_end:.3f} rad | Complementary={c_end:.3f} rad (tau_c={tau_c:.3f} s)")
```

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Using `math.asin(ay / 9.81)` instead of `math.atan2(ay, az)`:** If a vibration bump momentarily makes `ay = 9.90 m/s^2`, `ay / 9.81 > 1.0` crashes `math.asin()` with a `ValueError: math domain error`! `math.atan2(ay, az)` works safely for any finite acceleration vector.
- **Picking weights that do not add up to `1.0` (e.g., `0.98` and `0.05`):** If the gyro and accelerometer weights sum to `1.03` instead of `1.00`, the filter multiplies the angle by `1.03` on every single tick and diverges to infinity! Always write `(1.0 - alpha)` for the second weight.

## Recap
- **Gyroscope integration** is clean at high speed but drifts at $b_g \cdot t$; **accelerometer tilt** $\theta_{\text{acc}} = \operatorname{atan2}(a_y, a_z)$ is drift-free but sensitive to vibration.
- A **complementary IMU filter** updates $\hat{\theta}_k = \alpha(\hat{\theta}_{k-1} + \omega_{\text{gyro}}\Delta t) + (1-\alpha)\theta_{\text{acc},k}$ with crossover time constant $\tau_c = \frac{\alpha\Delta t}{1-\alpha}$.
