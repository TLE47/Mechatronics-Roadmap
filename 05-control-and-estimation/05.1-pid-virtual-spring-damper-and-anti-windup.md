## Goal
Simulate a closed-loop **PID controller** on a gravity-loaded robot arm joint, connecting **Proportional ($K_p$)** and **Derivative ($K_d$)** gains to a physical virtual spring and viscous damper, and adding **anti-windup clamping** to the **Integral ($K_i$)** accumulator so actuator saturation never causes violent windup overshoot.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **PD control as a virtual mechanical spring ($K_p$) and virtual viscous damper ($K_d$)**: How $u_{\text{PD}} = K_p(r - q) - K_d \dot{q}$ turns a motor into a programmable spring ($K_p$) with shock-absorber damping ($K_d$), and why pure PD sags by $e_{\text{ss}} = \tau_g / K_p$ under a steady gravity torque $\tau_g$.
  2. **Integral gravity sag elimination ($K_i$) and anti-windup clamping under actuator saturation**: How accumulating error $I_{\text{new}} = I + K_i e \Delta t$ eliminates steady-state gravity sag, and why clamping $I \in [-u_{\max}, +u_{\max}]$ stops **integrator windup** when the motor hits its voltage or torque limit.

## Learn

### Idea 1: PD control as a virtual mechanical spring ($K_p$) and virtual viscous damper ($K_d$)
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Proportional-integral-derivative controller ("Control loop example" & "Controller theory")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional%E2%80%93integral%E2%80%93derivative_controller) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Fundamental operation`**, **`Proportional term`**, and **`Derivative term`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip Laplace transfer function pole-zero derivations.
  - **Key takeaway:** In `Lesson 00.2`, a physical spring and damper pulled a mass toward equilibrium with $F = -kx - bv$. A **PD controller** creates that exact spring and shock absorber in software around any target angle $r$: the **Proportional term** $K_p(r - q)$ pulls toward $r$ like a virtual spring, while the **Derivative term** $-K_d \dot{q}$ pushes against velocity like viscous honey so the arm stops smoothly without ringing. Under a constant gravity load $\tau_g$, a pure virtual spring stretches until $K_p e_{\text{ss}} = \tau_g$, leaving a steady-state droop $e_{\text{ss}} = \tau_g / K_p$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Wikipedia — Damping ("Critical damping")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damping) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Review how critical damping ($K_d = 2\sqrt{J K_p}$) prevents overshoot.

### Idea 2: Integral gravity sag elimination ($K_i$) and anti-windup clamping under actuator saturation
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Integral windup ("Occurrence" & "Solutions")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral_windup) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Integral windup`**, **`Occurrence`**, and **`Solutions`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip multi-loop chemical process cascades.
  - **Key takeaway:** Adding the **Integral term** ($I \leftarrow I + K_i e \Delta t$) steadily builds up holding torque until gravity sag $e_{\text{ss}}$ reaches $0.000\text{ rad}$. However, every real motor driver saturates at a maximum effort $\pm u_{\max}$. During a large step move while the motor is already maxed out at $+u_{\max}$, an unclamped integrator keeps ballooning (**integrator windup**), forcing the arm to overshoot violently past the target! **Anti-windup clamping** freezes or clamps the integral accumulator whenever the actuator is saturated in the direction of the error.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Åström & Murray — Feedback Systems (fbsbook.org)](https://www.fbsbook.org/) — Chapter 11 covers PID physical intuition and anti-windup. The host answers `500` to scripts as of 2026-10-19 (site outage), so open it in a browser.

### Bridge Notes
Inside your $1\text{ kHz}$ timer `ISR` (`Lesson 04.1`), a **PID controller** computes tracking error $e = r - q$ and combines a **virtual mechanical spring ($K_p$)**, a **virtual viscous damper ($K_d$)** applied directly to velocity $\dot{q}$ (so sudden step changes in $r$ never cause a derivative kick), and an **integral accumulator ($I$)** with **anti-windup clamping** against actuator limit $\pm u_{\max}$:

$$
u_{\text{raw}} = \underbrace{K_p (r - q)}_{\text{Virtual Spring}} - \underbrace{K_d \dot{q}}_{\text{Virtual Damper}} + \underbrace{I}_{\text{Gravity Bias}}, \qquad u = \operatorname{clip}(u_{\text{raw}}, -u_{\max}, +u_{\max})
$$

Why does **anti-windup clamping** matter so much on hardware? When lifting a robot arm against gravity torque $\tau_g = 0.60\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ with actuator limit $u_{\max} = 1.20\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$:
1. **Pure PD ($K_i = 0$):** Settles cleanly without overshoot, but sags below the $1.000\text{ rad}$ target at $q_{\text{ss}} = 1.000 - \frac{\tau_g}{K_p} = 1.000 - \frac{0.60}{4.0} = 0.850\text{ rad}$ ($\text{sag} = 0.150\text{ rad}$).
2. **Unclamped PID ($K_i = 12.0$, no anti-windup):** Eliminates sag, but winds up while $u_{\text{raw}} > 1.20\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$, causing a massive **$0.372\text{ rad}$ overshoot**!
3. **Anti-windup PID ($K_i = 12.0$, clamped):** Eliminates steady-state sag ($q = 1.000\text{ rad}$) while cutting overshoot nearly $8\times$ down to **$0.047\text{ rad}$**.

def sim_pid_arm(kp, ki, kd, anti_windup):
    J, b, tau_g, u_max, dt = 0.02, 0.05, 0.60, 1.20, 0.001
    q, dq, i_term, max_q = 0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0

    for _ in range(3000):
        e = r - q
        i_candidate = i_term + ki * e * dt
        if anti_windup:
            i_candidate = max(-u_max, min(u_max, i_candidate))
        u_raw = kp * e - kd * dq + i_candidate
        if anti_windup and abs(u_raw) > u_max and (e * u_raw > 0.0):
            u_raw = kp * e - kd * dq + i_term

        ddq = (u - b * dq - tau_g) / J
        dq += ddq * dt
        q += dq * dt
        max_q = max(max_q, q)
    return q, max(0.0, max_q - r)

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Differentiating error `(e - e_prev) / dt` instead of position `-(q - q_prev) / dt`:** When target `r` steps from `0.0` to `1.0` in a single $1\text{ ms}$ tick, `(e - e_prev) / dt` jumps to $+1000\text{ rad/s}$ (**derivative kick**), slamming the motor into saturation! Always apply `-kd * dq` on measured velocity.
- **Enabling high $K_i$ before tuning $K_p$ and $K_d$:** Always set $K_i = 0$ first, tune $K_p$ and $K_d$ until step responses are crisp and damped, and only then add a small $K_i$ with anti-windup enabled.

## Check yourself

1. **PD gravity sag:** If a robot arm experiences a constant gravity torque $\tau_g = 0.60\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ and you double the proportional gain from $K_p = 4.0$ to $K_p = 8.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$ (with $K_i = 0$), what is the new steady-state gravity sag $e_{\text{ss}} = \tau_g / K_p$?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   $e_{\text{ss}} = \frac{0.60}{8.0} = \mathbf{0.075\text{ rad}}$ (doubling stiffness cuts steady-state sag in half).
   </details>

2. **Critical damping gain $K_d$:** For a frictionless joint with inertia $J = 0.02\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and virtual spring stiffness $K_p = 8.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$, what virtual damper gain $K_d = 2\sqrt{J K_p}$ achieves critical damping ($\zeta = 1.0$)?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   $K_d = 2\sqrt{0.02 \times 8.0} = 2\sqrt{0.16} = 2 \times 0.40 = \mathbf{0.80\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}\cdot\text{s/rad}}$.
   </details>

## Recap
- **PD control** acts as a **virtual mechanical spring ($K_p$)** plus a **virtual viscous damper ($K_d$)**, leaving steady-state sag $e_{\text{ss}} = \tau_g / K_p$ under constant load $\tau_g$.
- Adding **Integral action ($K_i$)** drives steady-state sag to zero, and **anti-windup clamping** freezes the integrator during actuator saturation to prevent windup overshoot.
