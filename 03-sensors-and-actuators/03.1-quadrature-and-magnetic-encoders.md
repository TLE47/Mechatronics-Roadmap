# Lesson 03.1: Quadrature Encoders ($4\times$ Counting) and 12-Bit Magnetic Angle Sensors

## Goal
Calculate shaft angle and angular resolution from an **incremental quadrature encoder** (`A/B` channels decoded at $4\times$ edges per slot) and a **12-bit absolute magnetic encoder** (`AS5600`, `0..4095` counts per revolution), and convert raw sensor counts into degrees and radians in Python.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Incremental quadrature encoders (`A/B` channels and $4\times$ edge counting)**: How two square-wave channels shifted by a quarter cycle let hardware count $4\times$ edges per disk slot ($\text{CPR} = 4 \cdot N_{\text{lines}}$) and tell clockwise from counter-clockwise rotation.
  2. **12-bit absolute magnetic encoders (`AS5600` `0..4095` counts to radians and degrees)**: How an on-axis diametric magnet above a Hall sensor IC reports instant absolute shaft angle in $2^{12} = 4096$ steps ($0.0879^\circ$ per count) right at power-on without homing.

## Learn

### Idea 1: Incremental quadrature encoders (`A/B` channels and $4\times$ edge counting)
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Incremental encoder ("Quadrature outputs")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incremental_encoder) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Incremental encoder`** and **`Quadrature outputs`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip linear glass scale interferometers.
  - **Key takeaway:** A single slotted optical sensor only tells you speed, not direction. An **incremental quadrature encoder** places two sensors (`Channel A` and `Channel B`) a quarter-slot apart ($90^\circ$ phase shift). When `A` rises before `B`, the shaft is turning forward (`+1`); when `B` rises before `A`, it is turning backward (`-1`). By counting rising and falling edges on both `A` and `B` (**$4\times$ quadrature decoding**), a disk with $N_{\text{lines}}$ slots plus a gearbox of ratio $N_{\text{gear}}$ yields $\text{CPR}_{\text{out}} = 4 \cdot N_{\text{lines}} \cdot N_{\text{gear}}$ counts per output revolution.
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun — Rotary Encoder Hookup Guide](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/rotary-encoder-hookup-guide) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Introduction`** and **`How Rotary Encoders Work`**.

### Idea 2: 12-bit absolute magnetic encoders (`AS5600` `0..4095` counts to radians and degrees)
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Rotary encoder ("Absolute encoder" & "Magnetic")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotary_encoder) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Absolute encoder`** and **`Magnetic`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip Gray-code mechanical contact brushes.
  - **Key takeaway:** Because an incremental encoder only counts changes since boot, it wakes up at `count = 0` no matter where the robot arm is pointing. An **absolute magnetic encoder** (`AS5600`, 12-bit `0..4095`) measures the horizontal magnetic field of a diametrically magnetized cylinder magnet (North on left half, South on right half) glued to the end of the shaft. With $2^{12} = 4096$ steps per full turn, raw count $c \in [0, 4095]$ maps directly to $\theta_{\text{deg}} = \frac{c}{4096} \times 360^\circ$ and $\theta_{\text{rad}} = \frac{c}{4096} \times 2\pi$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [MathsIsFun — Binary Number System](https://www.mathsisfun.com/binary-number-system.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Review how 12 binary bits represent $2^{12} = 4096$ distinct states.

### Bridge Notes
Choosing between an **incremental quadrature encoder** and a **12-bit absolute magnetic encoder** depends on whether your joint needs instant startup pose knowledge without a mechanical homing switch. On a brushed DC gearmotor with a rear-shaft optical disk of $N_{\text{lines}} = 500$ lines and a $N_{\text{gear}} = 10:1$ gearbox, counting every rising and falling edge on both `Channel A` and `Channel B` (**$4\times$ counting**) gives:

$$
\text{CPR}_{\text{out}} = 4 \cdot N_{\text{lines}} \cdot N_{\text{gear}} = 4 \times 500 \times 10 = 20000\text{ counts/rev}, \qquad \Delta\theta_{\text{deg}} = \frac{360^\circ}{\text{CPR}_{\text{out}}}
$$

By contrast, an on-axis **12-bit absolute magnetic encoder** (`AS5600`) mounted directly on the output joint shaft divides one mechanical turn into $2^{12} = 4096$ counts (`0..4095`; note that the full-circle divisor is $2^{12} = 4096$ because count `4096` wraps back to `0` at $360^\circ$):

$$
\theta_{\text{deg}} = \left(\frac{\text{raw}}{4096}\right) \cdot 360^\circ, \qquad \theta_{\text{rad}} = \left(\frac{\text{raw}}{4096}\right) \cdot 2\pi
$$

When mounting an `AS5600` in your hardware build, always verify two mechanical details: first, use a **diametrically magnetized** cylinder magnet (North and South split across the circular face) rather than a standard axial magnet whose field does not rotate with the shaft; second, tie the `DIR` pin explicitly to `GND` or `VCC` so clockwise rotation always increments the angle cleanly.

```python
import math


def quadrature_resolution(disk_lines, gear_ratio):
    cpr_out = 4 * disk_lines * gear_ratio
    deg_per_count = 360.0 / cpr_out
    return cpr_out, deg_per_count


def as5600_to_angle(raw_count):
    angle_deg = (raw_count / 4096.0) * 360.0
    angle_rad = (raw_count / 4096.0) * (2.0 * math.pi)
    return angle_deg, angle_rad


cpr, res_deg = quadrature_resolution(disk_lines=500, gear_ratio=10)
print(f"Quadrature 4x output CPR: {cpr} counts/rev | Resolution: {res_deg:.4f} deg/count")

    deg, rad = as5600_to_angle(raw)
```

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Using an axially magnetized magnet above an `AS5600`:** Standard fridge or craft magnets have North on the top face and South on the bottom face, so spinning the shaft does not rotate the horizontal field vector across the chip. Always buy a **diametrically magnetized** cylinder magnet.
- **Dividing a wrapped rotary encoder by `4095` instead of `4096`:** Unlike a linear ADC potentiometer that stops at $5.0\text{ V}$ (`1023`), a $360^\circ$ rotary encoder wraps around in a circle so step `4096` is the exact same angle as step `0`. Quarter turns occur at exact powers of two: `1024` ($90^\circ$), `2048` ($180^\circ$), and `3072` ($270^\circ$).
