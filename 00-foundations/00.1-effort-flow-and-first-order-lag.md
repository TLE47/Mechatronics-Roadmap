# Lesson 00.1: Cross-Domain Power ($P = \text{Effort} \times \text{Flow}$) and First-Order Lag

## Goal

- **New ideas (max 2):**

  - **Key takeaway:** In Stage 0 (`Lesson S0.4`), you learned that electrical power in Watts (`W`) is $P_{\text{elec}} = V \cdot I$ (Voltage push $\times$ Current flow). Mechanical systems obey the exact same law! **Mechanical power** for straight-line motion is $P_{\text{lin}} = F \cdot v$ (Force in `N` $\times$ velocity in `m/s`), and for rotating shafts it is $P_{\text{rot}} = \tau \cdot \omega$ (Torque in `N·m` $\times$ angular velocity in `rad/s`—**always in radians per second, never RPM!**). Together, these are called **Effort and Flow** variables: across any motor or gearbox, electrical power in equals mechanical power out plus heat loss.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Math is Fun — Power](https://www.mathsisfun.com/physics/power.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Power`** and **`Power Formulas`**.

### Idea 2: First-order lag step response and time constant $\tau$ ($63.2\%$ at $1\tau$, $99.3\%$ at $5\tau$)
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Capacitors ("Charging and Discharging")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/capacitors) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Review exact heading **`Charging and Discharging`** and notice how the rate of charging slows down as the capacitor voltage approaches the target supply voltage.
  - **What to skip:** Skip series/parallel capacitor bank math.
  - **Key takeaway:** In `Lesson S0.6`, you saw an RC capacitor charge $63.2\%$ of the way to $5.0\text{ V}$ in one time constant $\tau = R \cdot C$ and $99.3\%$ in $5\tau$. That exact curve is called a **first-order lag**, and it appears everywhere in mechatronics: a temperature sensor warming up, a motor spinning up against friction, or a software smoothing filter. At every tiny time step $\Delta t$, the state $y$ steps toward `target` by $\Delta y = \frac{\text{target} - y}{\tau} \cdot \Delta t$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [HyperPhysics — Work, Energy and Power](http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/work.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Work`**, **`Energy`**, and **`Power`**.

### Bridge Notes
Mechatronics unifies electronics and mechanics because **Effort and Flow** multiply to give instantaneous power in **Watts (`W`)** across every physical domain. In an electrical circuit, voltage $V$ (`V`) is the effort that pushes current $I$ (`A`), producing electrical power $P_{\text{elec}} = V \cdot I$. In straight-line motion, force $F$ (`N`) pushes at linear velocity $v$ (`m/s`), producing **mechanical power** $P_{\text{lin}} = F \cdot v$. In a rotating shaft, torque $\tau$ (`N·m`) twists at angular velocity $\omega$ (`rad/s`), producing rotational **mechanical power** $P_{\text{rot}} = \tau \cdot \omega$:

| Energy Domain | Effort ("Push") | Flow ("Movement") | Instantaneous Power ($P = \text{Effort} \times \text{Flow}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electrical** | Voltage $V$ (`V`) | Current $I$ (`A`) | $P_{\text{elec}} = V \cdot I$ (`W`) |
| **Linear Mechanical** | Force $F$ (`N`) | Velocity $v$ (`m/s`) | $P_{\text{lin}} = F \cdot v$ (`W`) |
| **Rotational Mechanical** | Torque $\tau$ (`N·m`) | Angular Velocity $\omega$ (`rad/s`) | $P_{\text{rot}} = \tau \cdot \omega$ (`W`) |

Similarly, whenever a system's rate of change (slope $\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta t}$ from `Lesson P.11`) is proportional to the remaining gap between `target` and current value $y$, it obeys the **first-order lag** update rule with time constant $\tau_{\text{lag}}$ (in seconds):

$$
\text{rate} = \frac{\text{target} - y}{\tau_{\text{lag}}}, \qquad y_{\text{new}} = y + \text{rate} \cdot \Delta t
$$

Whether $y$ is capacitor voltage ($\tau_{\text{lag}} = R C$), motor housing temperature, or unloaded motor speed, stepping this rule forward from $y(0) = 0$ reaches **$63.2\%$ of `target` at $t = 1\tau_{\text{lag}}$** and **$99.3\%$ of `target` at $t = 5\tau_{\text{lag}}$**.

def cross_domain_power(volts, amps, torque_nm, omega_rad_s):
    p_elec = volts * amps
    p_mech = torque_nm * omega_rad_s
    efficiency_pct = (p_mech / p_elec) * 100.0

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Multiplying torque $\tau$ (`N·m`) by speed in `RPM` instead of `rad/s`:** $P = \tau \cdot \omega$ ONLY works in base SI units where $\omega$ is in **radians per second (`rad/s`)**. As you learned in `Lesson P.10`, always convert $\text{RPM}$ to $\text{rad/s}$ first via $\omega = \text{RPM} \times \frac{2\pi}{60}$.
- **Choosing a simulation step size $\Delta t$ larger than the time constant $\tau_{\text{lag}}$:** If $\tau_{\text{lag}} = 0.5\text{ s}$ and you pick $\Delta t = 1.0\text{ s}$, a single Euler step overshoots past $100\%$ to $200\%$! Always choose $\Delta t \le \frac{\tau_{\text{lag}}}{50}$ so the discrete loop tracks the smooth curve accurately.

## Check yourself

1. **Rotational mechanical power:** A robot wheel delivers $\tau = 2.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ of torque while spinning at $\omega = 12.0\text{ rad/s}$. How many Watts (`W`) of mechanical power is the wheel delivering, and what is the minimum current it must draw from a $12.0\text{ V}$ battery if the motor is $100\%$ efficient?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   $P_{\text{mech}} = \tau \cdot \omega = 2.5 \times 12.0 = \mathbf{30.0\text{ W}}$. From a $12.0\text{ V}$ battery, $I = \frac{P}{V} = \frac{30.0\text{ W}}{12.0\text{ V}} = \mathbf{2.5\text{ A}}$.
   </details>

2. **First-order lag settling time:** A motor's speed responds to a step voltage command as a first-order lag with time constant $\tau_{\text{lag}} = 0.08\text{ s}$ ($80\text{ ms}$). How long does it take to reach $63.2\%$ of its final speed, and how long to reach $99.3\%$ ($5\tau_{\text{lag}}$)?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - $63.2\%$ at $1\tau_{\text{lag}} = \mathbf{0.08\text{ s}}$ ($80\text{ ms}$).
   - $99.3\%$ at $5\tau_{\text{lag}} = 5 \times 0.08\text{ s} = \mathbf{0.40\text{ s}}$ ($400\text{ ms}$).
   </details>

## Recap
- **Instantaneous power (`W`)** in any physical domain is $\text{Effort} \times \text{Flow}$: $P = V \cdot I = F \cdot v = \tau \cdot \omega$ (with $\omega$ in `rad/s`).
- A **first-order lag** steps via $y_{\text{new}} = y + \frac{\text{target} - y}{\tau} \Delta t$, reaching $63.2\%$ in $1\tau$ and $99.3\%$ in $5\tau$.
