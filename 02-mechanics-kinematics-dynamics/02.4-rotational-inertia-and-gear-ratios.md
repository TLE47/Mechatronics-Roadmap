## Goal
Calculate the **moment of inertia** ($I_{\text{rot}} = m r^2$) and **angular acceleration** ($\alpha = \tau_{\text{net}} / I_{\text{rot}}$) of a rotating link, and size a **gear ratio** ($N$) in Python by trading speed division ($\omega_{\text{out}} = \omega_{\text{in}} / N$) for torque multiplication ($\tau_{\text{out}} = \eta N \tau_{\text{in}}$) and $1/N^2$ **reflected inertia**.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Rotational Newton's Second Law ($\tau_{\text{net}} = I_{\text{rot}} \cdot \alpha$) and moment of inertia ($I_{\text{rot}} = m r^2$)**: Why rotational inertia depends on mass $m$ times the **square** of distance $r$ from the pivot (`kg·m^2`), relating net torque $\tau_{\text{net}}$ (`N·m`) to angular acceleration $\alpha$ (`rad/s^2`).
  2. **Gear reduction ratio ($N$): torque multiplication, speed division, and $N^2$ reflected inertia**: How meshing a small input gear ($N_{\text{in}}$ teeth) with a larger output gear ($N_{\text{out}}$ teeth, ratio $N = N_{\text{out}} / N_{\text{in}}$) multiplies torque by $\eta N$, divides speed by $N$, and shrinks the load's apparent inertia seen by the motor to $I_{\text{load}} / N^2$ (while introducing mechanical **backlash**).

## Learn

### Idea 1: Rotational Newton's Second Law ($\tau_{\text{net}} = I_{\text{rot}} \cdot \alpha$) and moment of inertia ($I_{\text{rot}} = m r^2$)
- **Primary resource:** [HyperPhysics — Moment of Inertia](http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/mi.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Rotational-Linear Parallels`** and **`Moment of Inertia`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip continuous volume integral derivations.
  - **Key takeaway:** Just as linear force accelerates a mass ($F_{\text{net}} = m \cdot a$), net rotational torque accelerates a spinning body according to **Rotational Newton's Second Law** $\tau_{\text{net}} = I_{\text{rot}} \cdot \alpha$, where $\alpha$ is **angular acceleration** (in **`rad/s^2`**) and $I_{\text{rot}}$ is the **moment of inertia** (rotational sluggishness, in **`kg·m^2`**). For a point mass $m$ at radius $r$ from the pivot, $I_{\text{rot}} = m \cdot r^2$. Notice the $r^2$: moving a heavy motor or gripper twice as far from the shoulder pivot makes its rotational inertia **$4\times$ larger**!
  - **Alternative resource:** [PhET — Torque](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/torque) `[verify in-app]` — Open the **`Moment of Inertia`** tab `[verify in-app]` and watch how moving mass outward increases $I_{\text{rot}} = m r^2$ and slows down angular acceleration $\alpha$.

### Idea 2: Gear reduction ratio ($N$): torque multiplication, speed division, and $N^2$ reflected inertia
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Gear train ("Gear ratio" & "Mechanical advantage")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_train) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Gear train`**, **`Gear ratio`**, and **`Mechanical advantage`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip epicyclic planetary carrier velocity tables (covered in Pass 2).
  - **Key takeaway:** Small DC motors spin very fast ($3{,}000\text{–}10{,}000\text{ RPM}$) with very little torque ($\sim 0.02\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$), whereas a robot arm needs slow, smooth speed and high torque. A **gearbox** with **gear ratio** $N = \frac{N_{\text{out}}}{N_{\text{in}}}$ (for example, $N = 50:1$) trades speed for torque by conserving mechanical power ($P = \tau \cdot \omega$ from `Lesson 00.1`):
    - **Speed divides by $N$:** $\omega_{\text{out}} = \frac{\omega_{\text{motor}}}{N}$
    - **Torque multiplies by $\eta \cdot N$:** $\tau_{\text{out}} = \eta \cdot N \cdot \tau_{\text{motor}}$ (where $\eta \approx 0.85$ is gearbox efficiency)
    - **Reflected inertia scales by $N^2$:** Because the motor shaft spins $N$ times faster than the load AND has $N$ times the leverage, the load inertia felt by the motor is divided by $N^2$ ($I_{\text{reflected}} = \frac{I_{\text{load}}}{N^2}$), or equivalently the motor's rotor inertia felt at the output joint is multiplied by $N^2$ ($I_{\text{joint}} = N^2 \cdot I_{\text{rotor}}$).
    - **Backlash:** Real gear teeth need a tiny gap so they don't jam; when the motor reverses direction, that free play (typically $0.5^\circ\text{–}2^\circ$ on hobby gearboxes) is called **backlash**.

### Bridge Notes
By **Rotational Newton's Second Law**, the net torque $\tau_{\text{net}}$ (`N·m`) acting on a joint equals its **moment of inertia** $I_{\text{rot}} = m \cdot r^2$ (`kg·m^2`) multiplied by its **angular acceleration** $\alpha$ (`rad/s^2`). When a small, fast motor drives a heavy robot arm through a gearbox with **gear reduction ratio** $N = N_{\text{out}} / N_{\text{in}}$, speed division, torque multiplication, and reflected inertia follow five fundamental equations in base SI units:

$$
I_{\text{rot}} = m \cdot r^2, \qquad \alpha = \frac{\tau_{\text{net}}}{I_{\text{rot}}}, \qquad \omega_{\text{out}} = \frac{\omega_{\text{motor}}}{N}, \qquad \tau_{\text{out}} = \eta \cdot N \cdot \tau_{\text{motor}}, \qquad I_{\text{load,reflected}} = \frac{I_{\text{load}}}{N^2}
$$

Why does **reflected inertia** scale with **$N^2$** instead of $N$?
Use kinetic energy conservation (`Lesson 00.1`)! A load with inertia $I_{\text{load}}$ spinning at $\omega_{\text{out}} = \frac{\omega_{\text{motor}}}{N}$ carries rotational kinetic energy:
- Load kinetic energy: $\frac{1}{2} I_{\text{load}} \omega_{\text{out}}^2 = \frac{1}{2} I_{\text{load}} \left(\frac{\omega_{\text{motor}}}{N}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{I_{\text{load}}}{N^2}\right) \omega_{\text{motor}}^2$.
- Thus, viewed from the fast-spinning motor shaft, the heavy arm feels **$N^2$ times lighter** ($I_{\text{load}} / N^2$)! Conversely, viewed from the outside world trying to push the robot arm by hand (backdriving the joint), the tiny motor rotor feels **$N^2$ times heavier** ($N^2 I_{\text{rotor}}$). With a $50:1$ gearbox, $N^2 = 2{,}500$!

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Forgetting to square the radius $r$ in $I_{\text{rot}} = m \cdot r^2$ or the gear ratio $N$ in reflected inertia:** Lever torque scales with $r^1$ (`Lesson P.12`), but rotational inertia scales with **$r^2$**, and reflected inertia through a gearbox scales with **$N^2$**.
- **Mounting a heavy elbow motor at the moving elbow joint instead of near the shoulder base:** Because $I_{\text{rot}} = m \cdot r^2$, putting a $0.3\text{ kg}$ motor out at the elbow ($r = 0.25\text{ m}$) burdens the shoulder with huge rotational inertia; mounting that motor near the shoulder ($r \approx 0$) and driving the elbow through a lightweight transmission (`Lesson 02.5`) slashes arm inertia!

## Check yourself

1. **Why mount heavy motors near the base?** If you move a $0.4\text{ kg}$ wrist motor from $r = 0.40\text{ m}$ inward to $r = 0.10\text{ m}$ (4 times closer to the shoulder pivot), by what factor does its moment of inertia $I_{\text{rot}} = m r^2$ around the shoulder decrease?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   Because $I_{\text{rot}} = m r^2$ depends on the **square** of $r$, reducing $r$ by $4\times$ reduces its moment of inertia by $4^2 = \mathbf{16\times}$!
   </details>

2. **Speed and torque through a $10:1$ reduction:** A motor spins at $\omega_{\text{motor}} = 200\text{ rad/s}$ with torque $\tau_{\text{motor}} = 0.10\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ into an ideal ($\eta = 1.0$) $N = 10:1$ gearbox. What are $\omega_{\text{out}}$ and $\tau_{\text{out}}$?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - Output speed: $\omega_{\text{out}} = \frac{200}{10} = \mathbf{20.0\text{ rad/s}}$.
   - Output torque: $\tau_{\text{out}} = 10 \times 0.10 = \mathbf{1.00\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$.
   </details>

## Recap
- **Rotational Newton's Second Law** is $\tau_{\text{net}} = I_{\text{rot}} \cdot \alpha$, where **moment of inertia** $I_{\text{rot}} = m r^2$ (`kg·m^2`) grows with the square of distance from the pivot.
- A **gear ratio** $N = N_{\text{out}} / N_{\text{in}}$ divides speed by $N$, multiplies torque by $\eta N$, and scales **reflected inertia** by $N^2$ ($I_{\text{reflected}} = I_{\text{load}} / N^2$).
