## Goal
Solve **2-link planar Inverse Kinematics (`IK`)** in Python to convert a desired **end-effector** target $(x, y)$ into the required shoulder and elbow motor angles $(\theta_1, \theta_2)$ using the **Law of Cosines** and `math.atan2(y, x)`, while clamping `-1.0..+1.0` at **kinematic singularities** ($r = L_1 + L_2$) and verifying the solution with a round-trip Forward Kinematics (`FK`) check.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Law of Cosines for elbow angle and reachability clamping**: Solving the triangle formed by $L_1$, $L_2$, and target distance $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ via $\cos\theta_2 = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - L_1^2 - L_2^2}{2 L_1 L_2}$, and clamping $\cos\theta_2 \in [-1.0, +1.0]$ so `math.acos` never crashes on floating-point rounding at the workspace edge.
  2. **Elbow-down vs. elbow-up geometric solutions and shoulder angle via `math.atan2(y, x)`**: Why $\theta_2 = +\arccos(\cos\theta_2)$ (`elbow-down`) and $\theta_2 = -\arccos(\cos\theta_2)$ (`elbow-up`) both reach the same $(x, y)$, and finding $\theta_1 = \text{atan2}(y, x) - \text{atan2}(L_2 \sin\theta_2, L_1 + L_2 \cos\theta_2)$.
- **New syntax:** `math.acos(val)` (inverse cosine returning `0..pi` radians).
- **Target time (unmeasured — [log yours](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/beginner-timing-log.md)):** Typical: 1.5–2.5 hours | Slow-learner: 3.0–4.0 hours

## Learn

### Idea 1: Law of Cosines for elbow angle and reachability clamping
- **Primary resource:** [Math is Fun — The Law of Cosines](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/trig-cosine-law.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`The Law of Cosines`**, **`When to Use`**, and **`Easier Version For Angles`** (solving for an angle when all three triangle side lengths are known).
  - **What to skip:** Skip memorization mnemonics at the bottom.
  - **Key takeaway:** When you command a robot hand to move to target $(x, y)$ at distance $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$ from the shoulder `(0, 0)`, the upper arm $L_1$, forearm $L_2$, and line $r$ form a triangle whose three side lengths are known! Applying the **Law of Cosines** gives the exterior elbow bend angle $\theta_2$: $\cos(\theta_2) = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - L_1^2 - L_2^2}{2 \cdot L_1 \cdot L_2}$.
    - **Reachability check:** A target $(x, y)$ is inside the reachable workspace if and only if $\lvert L_1 - L_2 \rvert \le r \le L_1 + L_2$ (which corresponds to $-1.0 \le \cos\theta_2 \le +1.0$).
    - **Why clamping `cos_t2` into `[-1.0, +1.0]` is mandatory:** Right on the outer **kinematic singularity** boundary where the arm is stretched completely straight ($r = L_1 + L_2$, $\theta_2 = 0^\circ$), tiny floating-point rounding (`Lesson P.6`) can make $\cos\theta_2 = 1.0000000000000002$, causing `math.acos(cos_t2)` to crash with `ValueError: math domain error`! Always clamp `cos_t2 = max(-1.0, min(1.0, cos_t2))` before calling `math.acos`.

### Idea 2: Elbow-down vs. elbow-up geometric solutions and shoulder angle via `math.atan2(y, x)`
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Inverse kinematics ("Analytical solutions to inverse kinematics")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverse_kinematics) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Inverse kinematics`**, **`Robotics`**, and **`Analytical solutions to inverse kinematics`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip Jacobian pseudoinverse matrices and 6-DOF spherical wrist sections (covered in Pass 2).
  - **Key takeaway:** Once $\theta_2 = +\arccos(\cos\theta_2)$ (**elbow-down**, $\theta_2 \ge 0$) or $\theta_2 = -\arccos(\cos\theta_2)$ (**elbow-up**, $\theta_2 \le 0$) is chosen, the shoulder angle $\theta_1$ is the target's overall ray angle $\text{atan2}(y, x)$ minus the internal triangle offset angle $\text{atan2}(L_2 \sin\theta_2, L_1 + L_2 \cos\theta_2)$, so $\theta_1 = \text{atan2}(y, x) - \text{atan2}(L_2 \sin\theta_2, L_1 + L_2 \cos\theta_2)$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Python 3 Docs — `math` ("Trigonometric functions")](https://docs.python.org/3/library/math.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Review `math.acos(x)` and `math.atan2(y, x)` under **`math — Mathematical functions`**.

### Bridge Notes
Let's trace the complete 3-step **Inverse Kinematics (`IK`)** algorithm that converts a desired Cartesian target $(x, y)$ into joint angles $(\theta_1, \theta_2)$ for link lengths $L_1, L_2$:
1. **Compute $\cos(\theta_2)$ via the Law of Cosines and apply reachability clamping to `[-1.0, +1.0]`:**
   Any reachable target $(x, y)$ satisfies $\lvert L_1 - L_2 \rvert \le \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} \le L_1 + L_2$. Even when $(x, y)$ sits right on the outer **kinematic singularity** boundary ($r = L_1 + L_2$), floating-point roundoff can produce $\cos(\theta_2) = 1.0000000000000002$, so we always clamp $\cos(\theta_2) = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - L_1^2 - L_2^2}{2 \cdot L_1 \cdot L_2}$ into `[-1.0, +1.0]` before calling `math.acos`.

2. **Choose between the elbow-down ($+\arccos$) and elbow-up ($-\arccos$) geometric solutions for $\theta_2$:**

$$
\theta_2 = +\arccos\!\big(\text{clamp}(\cos\theta_2, -1.0, +1.0)\big)
$$

3. **Compute shoulder angle $\theta_1$ using two-argument `math.atan2(y, x)`:**

$$
\theta_1 = \text{atan2}(y, x) - \text{atan2}\!\big(L_2 \sin(\theta_2),\ L_1 + L_2 \cos(\theta_2)\big)
$$

How do you prove your `ik_2link(x, y)` function is $100\%$ bug-free? Feed the resulting $(\theta_1, \theta_2)$ right back into your `fk_2link` function from `Lesson 02.2` and `assert` that the reconstructed $(x_{\text{fk}}, y_{\text{fk}})$ matches the target $(x, y)$ to within $10^{-6}\text{ m}$!

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **`ValueError: math domain error` inside `math.acos(cos_t2)` when the arm stretches straight out ($r = L_1 + L_2$):** Even for a valid boundary point, floating-point arithmetic can produce `1.0000000000000002`. Always clamp `cos_t2` into `[-1.0, +1.0]` before calling `math.acos`.
- **Switching randomly between `elbow-down` ($+\arccos$) and `elbow-up` ($-\arccos$) from one millisecond to the next:** Pick one branch (such as `elbow-down`) and stick with it across the trajectory so the arm does not violently whip across the straight-arm singularity.

## Check yourself

1. **Elbow-down vs. Elbow-up for `(0.15 m, 0.15 m)`:** For $L_1 = 0.15\text{ m}$ and $L_2 = 0.15\text{ m}$, the `elbow-down` solution for target $(0.15\text{ m}, 0.15\text{ m})$ is $(\theta_1 = 0^\circ, \theta_2 = +90^\circ)$. What is the `elbow-up` solution ($\theta_2 = -90^\circ$)?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   With $\theta_2 = -90^\circ$, $\theta_1 = \text{atan2}(0.15, 0.15) - \text{atan2}(-0.15, 0.15) = +45^\circ - (-45^\circ) = \mathbf{+90^\circ}$. That puts the elbow straight up at $(0, +0.15)$ and bends the forearm $90^\circ$ clockwise (`-90 deg`) to land at the exact same tip $(+0.15, +0.15)$!
   </details>

2. **What is a kinematic singularity?** At $\theta_2 = 0^\circ$ (arm stretched completely straight along $+x$ at $(0.30\text{ m}, 0.00\text{ m})$), why can't the tip move any farther in the $+x$ direction?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   At $\theta_2 = 0^\circ$, both links are lined up along the $+x$ axis (**kinematic singularity** at the workspace boundary), so rotating either joint at that instant only sweeps the tip vertically ($\pm y$) and cannot extend $+x$ any farther.
   </details>

## Recap
- **2-link Inverse Kinematics (`IK`)** uses the **Law of Cosines** ($\cos\theta_2 = \frac{x^2 + y^2 - L_1^2 - L_2^2}{2 L_1 L_2}$, clamped to `[-1, +1]`) to find the elbow angle $\theta_2$ and `math.atan2` to find the shoulder angle $\theta_1$.
- Every interior workspace point has two solutions (**elbow-down** $+\arccos$ and **elbow-up** $-\arccos$) that merge at the straight-arm **kinematic singularity** ($\theta_2 = 0^\circ$).
