# Lesson 02.2: 2-Link Planar Arm Forward Kinematics ($\theta_1, \theta_2 \rightarrow x, y$)

## Goal
Add a second link ($L_2$) and an elbow motor ($\theta_2$) to your planar robot arm, relate the **relative joint angle** $\theta_2$ to the forearm's **absolute world angle** ($\theta_{12} = \theta_1 + \theta_2$), and compute the **end-effector** (hand) position $(x, y)$ by chaining two tip-to-tail vectors in Python.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Absolute link angle ($\theta_{12} = \theta_1 + \theta_2$) from relative elbow joint angle $\theta_2$**: Because the elbow motor is bolted to the end of Link 1, turning the shoulder by $\theta_1$ rotates the forearm too—so the forearm's angle relative to the world $+x$ axis is $\theta_1 + \theta_2$.
  2. **Chaining tip-to-tail vectors for 2-link forward kinematics**: Adding the upper-arm vector $(L_1 \cos\theta_1, L_1 \sin\theta_1)$ and the forearm vector $(L_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2), L_2 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2))$ to locate both the elbow and the **end-effector** tip $(x, y)$.
- **New syntax:** None (`math.cos`, `math.sin`, `math.radians` from Track P).
- **Target time (unmeasured — [log yours](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/beginner-timing-log.md)):** Typical: 1.5–2.0 hours | Slow-learner: 2.5–3.5 hours

## Learn

### Idea 1: Absolute link angle ($\theta_{12} = \theta_1 + \theta_2$) from relative elbow joint angle $\theta_2$
- **Primary resource:** [Math is Fun — Angles](https://www.mathsisfun.com/angles.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Names of Angles`** and **`Positive and Negative Angles`** (`+CCW` vs. `-CW`).
  - **What to skip:** Skip reflex angle naming quizzes.
  - **Key takeaway:** On a real 2-link robot arm, the elbow encoder measures the **relative joint angle** $\theta_2$—how much the forearm bends relative to the straight line of Link 1 ($\theta_2 = 0^\circ$ means the elbow is completely straight!). Because Link 1 is already tilted at angle $\theta_1$ relative to the world $+x$ axis, the forearm's **absolute angle** relative to the world $+x$ axis is the sum of both angles: $\theta_{12} = \theta_1 + \theta_2$.

### Idea 2: Chaining tip-to-tail vectors for 2-link forward kinematics
- **Primary resource:** [Math is Fun — Vectors ("Adding Vectors")](https://www.mathsisfun.com/algebra/vectors.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Vectors`**, **`Notation`**, and **`Adding Vectors`** (adding the $x$-components together and adding the $y$-components together).
  - **What to skip:** Skip vector multiplication (dot and cross products).
  - **Key takeaway:** A 2-link arm is simply two 1-link vectors chained **tip-to-tail**:
    1. **Shoulder to Elbow (`Link 1`, length $L_1$, world angle $\theta_1$):** $x_{\text{elbow}} = L_1 \cos(\theta_1)$ and $y_{\text{elbow}} = L_1 \sin(\theta_1)$.
    2. **Elbow to End-Effector (`Link 2`, length $L_2$, world angle $\theta_1 + \theta_2$):** $\Delta x_2 = L_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$ and $\Delta y_2 = L_2 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$.
    3. **Total End-Effector (hand/gripper tip) position $(x, y)$:** $x = L_1 \cos(\theta_1) + L_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$ and $y = L_1 \sin(\theta_1) + L_2 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$.

### Bridge Notes
In **2-link forward kinematics**, we find the **end-effector** position $(x, y)$ by **chaining tip-to-tail vectors**: first the upper-arm vector $(L_1 \cos\theta_1, L_1 \sin\theta_1)$ from the shoulder `(0, 0)` to the elbow $(x_1, y_1)$, and then the forearm vector $(L_2 \cos\theta_{12}, L_2 \sin\theta_{12})$ from the elbow to the hand.

Why is the angle inside the second cosine and sine the **absolute link angle** $\theta_{12} = \theta_1 + \theta_2$ instead of just the **relative joint angle** $\theta_2$?
Test it with an extreme-case sanity check (`Lesson 00.3`)! Suppose $L_1 = 0.15\text{ m}$ and $L_2 = 0.15\text{ m}$. Keep the elbow locked completely straight ($\theta_2 = 0^\circ$) and raise the shoulder straight up to $\theta_1 = +90^\circ$:
- With the **correct** formula $\theta_{12} = \theta_1 + \theta_2 = 90^\circ + 0^\circ = 90^\circ$, both links point straight up along $+y$, giving $(x, y) = (0.00\text{ m}, +0.30\text{ m})$.
- If you **forgot** to add $\theta_1$ and wrote $L_2 \cos(\theta_2) = L_2 \cos(0^\circ)$, your code would claim the forearm always points horizontally right regardless of how the shoulder moves!

| Posture ($L_1 = 0.15\text{ m}, L_2 = 0.15\text{ m}$) | $\theta_1$ (`deg`) | $\theta_2$ (`deg`) | Elbow $(x_1, y_1)$ (`m`) | End-Effector $(x, y)$ (`m`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Stretched Straight Right** | $0^\circ$ | $0^\circ$ | $(+0.150, +0.000)$ | $(+0.300, +0.000)$ |
| **L-Shape (Shoulder $0^\circ$, Elbow $+90^\circ$)** | $0^\circ$ | $+90^\circ$ | $(+0.150, +0.000)$ | $(+0.150, +0.150)$ |
| **Stretched Straight Up** | $+90^\circ$ | $0^\circ$ | $(+0.000, +0.150)$ | $(+0.000, +0.300)$ |

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Writing `l2_m * math.cos(t2)` instead of `l2_m * math.cos(t1 + t2)`:** The elbow angle $\theta_2$ is measured relative to Link 1, so its angle relative to the horizontal $+x$ world axis is always $\theta_1 + \theta_2$.
- **Converting $\theta_1 + \theta_2$ to radians *after* adding a radian and a degree:** Never mix radians and degrees in `t1 + t2`; convert both to radians first!

2. **Minimum and maximum reach of a 2-link arm:** If $L_1 = 0.20\text{ m}$ and $L_2 = 0.12\text{ m}$, what is the farthest distance $r_{\max}$ from `(0, 0)` the end-effector can reach ($\theta_2 = 0^\circ$), and what is the closest distance $r_{\min}$ when folded completely back ($\theta_2 = 180^\circ$)?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - Maximum reach: $r_{\max} = L_1 + L_2 = 0.20 + 0.12 = \mathbf{0.32\text{ m}}$.
   - Minimum reach: $r_{\min} = \lvert L_1 - L_2 \rvert = 0.20 - 0.12 = \mathbf{0.08\text{ m}}$.
   </details>

## Recap
- The elbow motor measures a **relative joint angle** $\theta_2$, so the forearm's absolute world angle is $\theta_{12} = \theta_1 + \theta_2$.
- **2-link forward kinematics** chains both link vectors tip-to-tail to locate the **end-effector**: $x = L_1 \cos\theta_1 + L_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$ and $y = L_1 \sin\theta_1 + L_2 \sin(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$.
