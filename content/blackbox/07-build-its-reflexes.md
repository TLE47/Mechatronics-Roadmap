The loop wandered. Under load it stretched from one millisecond to four, and the timing diagram looked like a bad heart.

We spent three weeks on it. Rewrote the scheduler twice. Bought a better clock.

It was real. It was also not why the demo failed. The loop was late, but the loop was not lying about the arm — the arm was already wrong before the loop got a say.

I am telling you this because finding a fault is not the same as finding the fault. When something is measurably bad, write it down, fix it if you can, and then keep asking what else is true.
