## Learn

### Idea 1: Closed DC circuits and ground reference (`0 V`)
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Voltage, Current, Resistance, and Ohm's Law](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/voltage-current-resistance-and-ohms-law) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Electricity Basics`**, **`Electrical Charge`**, **`Voltage`**, **`Current`**, and **`Resistance`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip **`Ohm's Law`** until Idea 2 below, and skip the bottom kit links.
  - **Key takeaway:** Think of a **Direct Current (`DC`)** circuit as a closed water pipe loop driven by a pump: **voltage** ($V$, in **Volts `V`**) is the pressure difference between two points, **current** ($I$, in **Amperes `A`**) is the flow rate of charge through the wire, and **resistance** ($R$, in **Ohms `Ω`**) is a narrow pipe constriction that restricts flow. Current only flows when there is an unbroken **closed loop** from the positive supply back to **ground (`GND`, defined as $0.0\text{ V}$)**.
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun — What is a Circuit?](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/what-is-a-circuit) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Circuit Basics`** and **`Voltage and How it Works`**.

### Idea 2: Ohm's Law relating voltage, current, and resistance
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Voltage, Current, Resistance, and Ohm's Law ("Ohm's Law")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/voltage-current-resistance-and-ohms-law) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Ohm's Law`** and **`An Ohm's Law Experiment`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip the bottom shopping section.
  - **Key takeaway:** **Ohm's Law** states that the voltage drop across a resistor equals the current flowing through it times its resistance: $V = I \cdot R$. Rearranging gives $I = \frac{V}{R}$ (doubling resistance cuts current in half) and $R = \frac{V}{I}$.
  - **Optional interactive extra:** [PhET — Ohm's Law Interactive Simulation](https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/ohms-law) `[verify in-app]` — Drag the **`Voltage`** and **`Resistance`** sliders `[verify in-app]` to watch current in `mA` scale with $V$ and $1/R$.

### Bridge Notes
In Stage 0, all circuits run on **Direct Current (`DC`)** standardized on a **$5.0\text{ V}$ supply** returning to **`GND`** (**ground**, the common $0.0\text{ V}$ reference point). Saying a node is at "$5.0\text{ V}$" always means *"5.0 Volts higher than `GND`."*

Remember from Lesson P.7 that **Ohm's Law** requires base SI units—**Volts (`V`)**, **Amperes (`A`)**, and **Ohms (`Ω`)**:

$$
V = I \cdot R, \qquad I = \frac{V}{R}, \qquad R = \frac{V}{I}
$$

For example, if you connect a $1.0\text{ k}\Omega$ ($1{,}000\ \Omega$) resistor across a $5.0\text{ V}$ supply and `GND`:
1. Convert $R = 1.0\text{ k}\Omega = 1{,}000\ \Omega$.
2. Compute current in Amperes: $I = \frac{5.0\text{ V}}{1{,}000\ \Omega} = 0.005\text{ A}$.
3. Multiply by $1{,}000$ to express in milliamps: $0.005\text{ A} \times 1{,}000 = 5.0\text{ mA}$.

If a wire breaks anywhere in the loop, the loop becomes an **open circuit** ($R = \infty$), and current drops immediately to $0.0\text{ A}$.

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Dividing Volts by `kΩ` and calling the result Amperes:** Dividing $5.0\text{ V}$ by $1{,}000\ \Omega$ gives $0.005\text{ A}$ ($5.0\text{ mA}$). If you divide $5.0 / 1.0 = 5.0$, remember that Volts divided by kilo-ohms gives **milliamps (`mA`)**, not Amperes! Converting to base Ohms (`1000.0`) first avoids confusion.
- **No return path to `GND`:** A single wire from `+5V` to a resistor with the other leg left unconnected has zero current flow because the loop is not closed back to `GND`.

## Check yourself

1. **Ohm's Law for current:** A $10\text{ k}\Omega$ ($10{,}000\ \Omega$) resistor is connected between `5V` and `GND`. How much current flows through it in Amperes (`A`) and in milliamps (`mA`)?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   $I = \frac{5.0\text{ V}}{10{,}000\ \Omega} = \mathbf{0.0005\text{ A}} = \mathbf{0.50\text{ mA}}$.
   </details>

2. **Ohm's Law for resistance:** You measure $10.0\text{ mA}$ ($0.010\text{ A}$) flowing from a $5.0\text{ V}$ supply through an unknown resistor to `GND`. What is the resistor's value in Ohms (`Ω`)?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   $R = \frac{V}{I} = \frac{5.0\text{ V}}{0.010\text{ A}} = \mathbf{500\ \Omega}$.
   </details>

3. **Effect of doubling resistance:** If you keep voltage fixed at $5.0\text{ V}$ and double the resistance from $500\ \Omega$ to $1{,}000\ \Omega$, what happens to the current?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   Current is **cut in half**, dropping from $10.0\text{ mA}$ to **$5.0\text{ mA}$**.
   </details>

## Recap
- **Voltage ($V$)** is electrical pressure difference relative to **ground (`GND`, $0\text{ V}$)**, **current ($I$)** is charge flow rate in Amperes (`A`), and **resistance ($R$)** opposes flow in Ohms (`Ω`).
- **Ohm's Law** ($V = I \cdot R$, $I = V/R$, $R = V/I$) links all three in any closed loop.
