## Goal
Measure circuit voltage and current in Falstad without blowing an ammeter fuse—placing **voltmeters in parallel** across components and **ammeters in series** inside the broken loop—and configure a **current-limited (`CC/CV`) bench power supply** so wiring mistakes fold voltage back safely instead of smoking parts.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Multimeter probing rules (voltmeter in parallel, ammeter in series, ohmmeter unpowered)**: Why a voltmeter has mega-ohm input resistance ($R_V \approx 10\text{ M}\Omega$) and goes **across** two nodes, whereas an ammeter has near-zero resistance ($R_A \approx 0.05\ \Omega$) and must be inserted **in series** by breaking the circuit.
  2. **Bench power supply Constant-Voltage (`CV`) versus Constant-Current (`CC`) current limiting**: Setting a safety current ceiling $I_{\text{limit}}$ on a power supply so that if load current reaches $I_{\text{limit}}$, the supply automatically switches from Constant Voltage (`CV`) to Constant Current (`CC`) and drops its output voltage to $V_{\text{out}} = I_{\text{limit}} \cdot R_{\text{load}}$.
- **New syntax:** Falstad Ammeter (`370`) and Voltage Probe (`p`) elements.
- **Target time (unmeasured — [log yours](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/beginner-timing-log.md)):** Typical: 1.0–1.5 hours | Slow-learner: 2.0–3.0 hours

## Learn

### Idea 1: Multimeter probing rules (voltmeter in parallel, ammeter in series, ohmmeter unpowered)
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — How to Use a Multimeter](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/how-to-use-a-multimeter) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Measuring Voltage`**, **`Measuring Resistance`**, **`Measuring Current`**, and **`Continuity`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip **`Changing the Fuse`** until you read the ammeter warning in the Bridge Notes below.
  - **Key takeaway:** A digital multimeter (`DMM`) combines three instruments with completely different internal circuits:
    1. **Voltmeter (`V` mode):** Has huge internal resistance ($\approx 10\text{ M}\Omega$). Touch its red and black probes **in parallel (across)** the component while power is ON.
    2. **Ammeter (`A` / `mA` mode):** Has near-zero internal resistance ($\approx 0.05\ \Omega$, like a bare wire!). You **must break the loop** and wire the ammeter **in series** so current flows *through* the meter. Touching an ammeter in parallel across `5V` and `GND` creates a dead short circuit and blows the meter's internal fuse!
    3. **Ohmmeter (`Ω` / Continuity mode):** Injects its own tiny test current; **always turn circuit power OFF** before measuring resistance or continuity.

### Idea 2: Bench power supply Constant-Voltage (`CV`) versus Constant-Current (`CC`) current limiting
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — How to Power a Project](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/how-to-power-a-project) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Overview`** and **`Variable DC Benchtop Power Supply`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip solar/LiPo charging sections (covered in Track 01).
  - **Key takeaway:** Unlike a raw battery that will happily dump $50\text{ A}$ into a wiring mistake and melt your breadboard, an adjustable **bench power supply** lets you set **two** knobs before turning power on: a target voltage $V_{\text{set}}$ (e.g., $5.0\text{ V}$) and a maximum current limit $I_{\text{limit}}$ (e.g., $0.10\text{ A} = 100\text{ mA}$ for a logic breadboard). As long as your circuit draws less than $I_{\text{limit}}$, the supply stays in **Constant-Voltage (`CV`)** mode at $5.0\text{ V}$. The instant a short circuit tries to draw more than $I_{\text{limit}}$, the supply switches to **Constant-Current (`CC`)** mode—clamping current to $I_{\text{limit}}$ and folding output voltage down to $V_{\text{out}} = I_{\text{limit}} \cdot R_{\text{load}}$!

### Bridge Notes
Why does a **voltmeter** connect in **parallel** while an **ammeter** connects in **series**?
- A **voltmeter** measures the potential difference *between* two nodes (`V_A - V_B`). Its internal resistance is huge ($\approx 10\text{ M}\Omega$), so touching its probes across a component draws almost zero current and does not disturb the circuit.
- An **ammeter** measures the flow rate *through* a branch. Its internal shunt resistance is tiny ($\approx 0.05\ \Omega$, like a bare wire) so it doesn't block the current it is trying to measure. Because $R_A \approx 0.05\ \Omega$, touching an ammeter in parallel across a $5.0\text{ V}$ supply creates a dead short circuit ($I = 5.0 / 0.05 = 100\text{ A}$) that instantly blows the meter's fuse!

Similarly, a **current-limited (`CC/CV`) bench power supply** acts like an automatic safety circuit breaker during bring-up:
- In **Constant-Voltage (`CV`) mode** ($I_{\text{load}} < I_{\text{limit}}$), the supply holds $V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{set}}$ ($5.00\text{ V}$).
- In **Constant-Current (`CC`) mode** ($V_{\text{set}} / R_{\text{load}} \ge I_{\text{limit}}$), the supply clamps current at $I = I_{\text{limit}}$ ($0.10\text{ A}$) and drops $V_{\text{out}} = I_{\text{limit}} \cdot R_{\text{load}}$ ($0.05\text{ V}$ across a $0.5\ \Omega$ accidental short), saving your components from smoke!

| Instrument / Supply Mode | How It Connects / Operates | Internal Resistance / Math | What Happens If You Do It Wrong |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Voltmeter (`V`)** | **In parallel** (across two nodes, black probe on `GND`) | $R_V \approx 10\text{ M}\Omega$ (draws $\approx 0\text{ A}$) | If wired in series, its $10\text{ M}\Omega$ resistance blocks circuit current. |
| **Ammeter (`mA` / `A`)** | **In series** (break the wire and insert meter in the path) | $R_A \approx 0.05\ \Omega$ (acts like a wire) | **If touched in parallel across `5V` and `GND`, it shorts the supply and blows the meter fuse!** |
| **Ohmmeter (`Ω`)** | Across component with **circuit power OFF** | Injects tiny test current | Measuring a live powered circuit gives bogus readings or damages the meter. |
| **Supply in `CV` Mode** | Normal operation ($I_{\text{load}} < I_{\text{limit}}$) | $V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{set}}$, $I = V_{\text{set}} / R_{\text{load}}$ | — |
| **Supply in `CC` Mode** | Current-limit protection ($V_{\text{set}} / R_{\text{load}} \ge I_{\text{limit}}$) | $I = I_{\text{limit}}$, $V_{\text{out}} = I_{\text{limit}} \cdot R_{\text{load}}$ | Protects your circuit! On a $0.5\ \Omega$ short with $I_{\text{limit}} = 0.10\text{ A}$, $V_{\text{out}}$ drops to $0.05\text{ V}$. |

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Leaving the red multimeter lead plugged into the `A` / `mA` current jack when switching the dial back to measure Voltage (`V`):** Even if the dial is on `V`, the `A` jack is wired directly through a low-resistance shunt to the `COM` jack! Touching the probes across a battery while the red lead is in the `A` jack shorts the battery through the meter. **Always move the red probe back to the `V/Ω` jack immediately after measuring current.**
- **Measuring resistance (`Ω`) while the circuit is still powered on:** Current from the circuit's own power supply confuses the ohmmeter and produces nonsensical readings (like negative resistance). Always disconnect power first.

## Check yourself

1. **Voltmeter vs. Ammeter connection:** To measure the voltage across an LED, do you connect the voltmeter in **series** or in **parallel** with the LED? What about measuring the current flowing through the LED with an ammeter?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - **Voltmeter:** Connect **in parallel** (across the LED's two legs).
   - **Ammeter:** Break the loop and connect **in series** with the LED and its resistor.
   </details>

2. **Why an ammeter across a battery blows the fuse:** An ammeter has internal resistance $R_A = 0.05\ \Omega$ protected by a $0.5\text{ A}$ ($500\text{ mA}$) fuse. If you accidentally touch the ammeter probes directly across a $5.0\text{ V}$ supply in parallel, how much current tries to flow through the meter?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   $I = \frac{5.0\text{ V}}{0.05\ \Omega} = \mathbf{100\text{ A}}$—which is $200\times$ higher than the $0.5\text{ A}$ fuse rating, blowing the fuse instantly!
   </details>

3. **`CC/CV` bench supply trace:** Your bench supply is set to $V_{\text{set}} = 12.0\text{ V}$ and $I_{\text{limit}} = 0.50\text{ A}$. What mode (`CV` or `CC`), voltage $V_{\text{out}}$, and current $I$ do you see when connected to (a) a $240\ \Omega$ resistor, and (b) a $4.0\ \Omega$ resistor?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - (a) Across $240\ \Omega$: $12.0 / 240 = 0.05\text{ A} < 0.50\text{ A}$, so **`CV` mode**, **$V_{\text{out}} = 12.0\text{ V}$**, **$I = 0.05\text{ A}$ ($50\text{ mA}$)**.
   - (b) Across $4.0\ \Omega$: $12.0 / 4.0 = 3.0\text{ A} \ge 0.50\text{ A}$, so **`CC` mode**, **$I = 0.50\text{ A}$**, and voltage drops to $V_{\text{out}} = 0.50\text{ A} \times 4.0\ \Omega = \mathbf{2.0\text{ V}}$.
   </details>

## Recap
- **Voltmeters** ($R_V \approx 10\text{ M}\Omega$) connect **in parallel**; **ammeters** ($R_A \approx 0.05\ \Omega$) connect **in series** inside a broken loop; **ohmmeters** require **power OFF**.
- A **current-limited (`CC/CV`) bench supply** stays at $V_{\text{set}}$ in `CV` mode during normal operation and folds voltage down to $I_{\text{limit}} \cdot R_{\text{load}}$ in `CC` mode to protect against shorts.
