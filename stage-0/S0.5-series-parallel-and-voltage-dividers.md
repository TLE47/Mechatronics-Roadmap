# Lesson S0.5: Series vs. Parallel Resistor Networks, Voltage Dividers and Potentiometers

## Goal
Distinguish **series** circuits (same current, voltages add) from **parallel** circuits (same voltage, currents add), and use the **voltage divider** formula ($V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}} \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$) to scale high sensor voltages down or read a 3-pin **potentiometer**.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Series versus parallel resistor networks**: Why series resistances add ($R_{\text{total}} = R_1 + R_2$) along a single current path while parallel branches share the full voltage and add their currents ($I_{\text{total}} = I_1 + I_2$).
  2. **Resistor voltage dividers and potentiometers**: Using two series resistors to produce an intermediate voltage $V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}} \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$, and how a potentiometer's movable **wiper** varies that ratio continuously from $0\%$ to $100\%$.
- **New syntax:** None (uses Python functions and arithmetic from Track P).

- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Series and Parallel Circuits](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/series-and-parallel-circuits) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`

  - **What to skip:** Skip complex multi-loop resistor reductions at the bottom of the page.
  - **Key takeaway:** Components wired in **series** (end-to-end along a single path) carry the **exact same current** $I$, and their resistances add together ($R_{\text{total}} = R_1 + R_2$) while the supply voltage splits across them. Components wired in **parallel** (side-by-side across the same two nodes) experience the **exact same voltage** $V$, and their branch currents add together ($I_{\text{total}} = I_1 + I_2$).

### Idea 2: Resistor voltage dividers and potentiometers
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Voltage Dividers](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/voltage-dividers) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Ideal Voltage Divider`**, **`The Circuit`**, **`The Equation`**, **`Simplifications`**, **`Applications`**, and **`Potentiometers`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip the loaded-divider derivation at the very bottom until Track 01.
  - **Key takeaway:** When two resistors $R_1$ (top, connected to $V_{\text{in}}$) and $R_2$ (bottom, connected to `GND`) are wired in series, the midpoint node between them sits at $V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}} \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$. A **potentiometer** is a single resistive track wired between `5V` and `GND` with a rotating middle pin (the **wiper**) that splits the track into $R_1$ and $R_2$, letting you dial $V_{\text{out}}$ smoothly from $0.0\text{ V}$ to $5.0\text{ V}$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Wokwi Docs — `wokwi-potentiometer` Reference](https://docs.wokwi.com/parts/wokwi-potentiometer) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Pin names`** (`GND`, `SIG`, `VCC`) and **`Using the Potentiometer in Arduino`**.

### Bridge Notes
Understanding **series** and **parallel** circuits comes down to two physical conservation rules (**Kirchhoff's Laws**):
1. **In a series loop**, charge has only one path, so current $I$ is identical everywhere ($I = \frac{V_{\text{in}}}{R_1 + R_2}$), and the individual voltage drops sum to the supply voltage ($V_{\text{in}} = V_1 + V_2$).
2. **In a parallel circuit**, every branch connects across the same two nodes, so every branch sees the full supply voltage $V_{\text{in}}$ while the supply current splits among the branches ($I_{\text{total}} = I_1 + I_2$).

Multiplying the series current $I = \frac{V_{\text{in}}}{R_1 + R_2}$ by the bottom resistor $R_2$ gives the **voltage divider** equation:

$$
V_{\text{out}} = I \cdot R_2 = V_{\text{in}} \cdot \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}
$$

For example, if you want an Arduino Uno pin (which safely reads $0\text{ V}$ to $5.0\text{ V}$) to monitor a $12.0\text{ V}$ robot battery, choosing $R_1 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$ on top and $R_2 = 4.7\text{ k}\Omega$ on the bottom scales $12.0\text{ V}$ down to $12.0 \times \frac{4700}{10000 + 4700} \approx 3.84\text{ V}$—safely below $5.0\text{ V}$! *(Simulator note: Test fixed-resistor dividers in **Falstad** (`falstad/S0.5-voltage-divider.txt`), and use Wokwi's **`wokwi-potentiometer`** when feeding an adjustable voltage to an Arduino Uno pin.)*

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Swapping $R_1$ (top) and $R_2$ (bottom) on a battery voltage divider:** Putting $4.7\text{ k}\Omega$ on top and $10\text{ k}\Omega$ on the bottom outputs $12.0 \times \frac{10}{14.7} = 8.16\text{ V}$—which exceeds $5.0\text{ V}$ and damages an Arduino Uno pin! Always measure the midpoint with a voltmeter before connecting it to `A0`.
- **Trying to power a heavy load (like a motor) from a resistor voltage divider:** A voltage divider is only for **low-current voltage sensing**; drawing heavy current from the midpoint pulls $V_{\text{out}}$ down immediately.

## Check yourself

1. **Equal-resistor divider:** Two identical $10\text{ k}\Omega$ resistors ($R_1 = 10\text{ k}\Omega, R_2 = 10\text{ k}\Omega$) are wired in series between `5.0 V` and `GND`. What is the midpoint voltage $V_{\text{out}}$?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   $V_{\text{out}} = 5.0\text{ V} \times \frac{10}{10 + 10} = 5.0 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{2.50\text{ V}}$.
   </details>

2. **Series vs. parallel resistance:** Two $1{,}000\ \Omega$ resistors wired in **series** have total resistance $2{,}000\ \Omega$. When those same two $1{,}000\ \Omega$ resistors are wired in **parallel** across `5.0 V` (drawing $5\text{ mA} + 5\text{ mA} = 10\text{ mA}$ total), what is their combined equivalent resistance $R_{\text{eq}} = \frac{5.0\text{ V}}{0.010\text{ A}}$?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   **$500\ \Omega$**! Two identical resistors in parallel provide two side-by-side paths for current, cutting total resistance in half.
   </details>

3. **Potentiometer wiring:** Which pin of a 3-pin potentiometer (`GND`, middle wiper `SIG`, or `VCC`) outputs the variable $0\text{–}5\text{ V}$ signal to an Arduino analog input pin?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   The **middle wiper pin (`SIG`)**, while the two outer pins connect to `5V` (`VCC`) and `GND`.
   </details>

## Recap
- **Series** resistors share the same current and add their resistances ($R_1 + R_2$); **parallel** branches share the same voltage and add their currents ($I_1 + I_2$).
- A **voltage divider** outputs $V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}}\frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$, and a **potentiometer** varies that ratio via its middle wiper pin.
