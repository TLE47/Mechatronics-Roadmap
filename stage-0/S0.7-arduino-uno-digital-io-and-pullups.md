# Lesson S0.7: Meet the Microcontroller (Arduino Uno `5 V`) — Digital I/O and `INPUT_PULLUP`

## Goal
Write your first **Arduino Uno (`5.0 V`)** sketch in Wokwi using `setup()` and `loop()` to drive a digital output (`pinMode`, `digitalWrite`) and read a pushbutton reliably without floating-pin noise using the internal pull-up resistor (`INPUT_PULLUP`, `digitalRead`).

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Microcontroller program loop (`setup`/`loop`) and digital output (`pinMode`/`digitalWrite`)**: How an Arduino runs `setup()` once at power-on and `loop()` forever, switching a digital pin between `LOW` ($0.0\text{ V}$) and `HIGH` ($5.0\text{ V}$, $\le 20\text{ mA}$ safe continuous limit).
  2. **Digital input (`digitalRead`) and active-low pushbuttons with `INPUT_PULLUP`**: Why an unconnected input pin "floats" randomly between `0` and `1`, and how enabling the internal $20\text{–}50\text{ k}\Omega$ pull-up resistor to `+5V` holds the pin `HIGH` (`1`) when released and `LOW` (`0`) when pressed to `GND`.
- **New syntax (C/C++ on Arduino Uno):** `void setup() { ... }`, `void loop() { ... }`, `int x = ...;` (semicolons `;` at the end of statements), `pinMode(pin, mode)`, `digitalWrite(pin, val)`, `digitalRead(pin)`, `Serial.begin(9600)`, `Serial.println(...)`.
- **Estimated time (untimed guesses):** Typical: 1.5–2.0 hours | Slow-learner: 2.5–3.5 hours

## Learn

### Idea 1: Microcontroller program loop (`setup`/`loop`) and digital output (`pinMode`/`digitalWrite`)
- **Primary resource:** [Wokwi Docs — `wokwi-arduino-uno` Reference](https://docs.wokwi.com/parts/wokwi-arduino-uno) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Pin names`** (`0` to `13`, `A0` to `A5`, `5V`, `GND`, `LED`), **`Simulation features`**, and **`Simulator examples`** (`Arduino Blink`).
  - **What to skip:** Skip `AREF` and `IOREF` until Track 04.
  - **Key takeaway:** We standardize all Stage 0 microcontroller lessons on the **Arduino Uno (`ATmega328P`, `5.0 V` logic)** in Wokwi because its single $5.0\text{ V}$ voltage domain, 14 digital pins (`0`–`13`), and 6 analog input pins (`A0`–`A5`) are the cleanest possible entry point. Every Arduino sketch has two functions: `void setup()` runs **once** at power-on (to configure pins with `pinMode(13, OUTPUT);`), and `void loop()` repeats **forever** (driving pins to $5.0\text{ V}$ with `digitalWrite(13, HIGH);` or $0.0\text{ V}$ with `digitalWrite(13, LOW);`, up to a $20\text{ mA}$ safe continuous pin limit).

### Idea 2: Digital input (`digitalRead`) and active-low pushbuttons with `INPUT_PULLUP`
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Pull-up Resistors](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/pull-up-resistors) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` & [Wokwi Docs — `wokwi-pushbutton` Reference](https://docs.wokwi.com/parts/wokwi-pushbutton) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** On the SparkFun page, read exact headings **`What is a Pull-up Resistor?`** and **`Calculating a Pull-up Resistor Value`**. On the Wokwi pushbutton page, read **`Pin names`** (`1.l`, `1.r`, `2.l`, `2.r`) and **`Connecting a pushbutton to an Arduino`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip open-drain I2C bus pull-up math at the bottom of the SparkFun page (covered in Track 02).
  - **Key takeaway:** If you wire a pushbutton to a digital input pin with `pinMode(2, INPUT);` and leave the pin unconnected when the button is not pressed, the pin **floats**—picking up stray electrostatic noise from the air and flipping randomly between `HIGH` (`1`) and `LOW` (`0`). Calling **`pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP);`** switches on a built-in resistor ($20\text{–}50\text{ k}\Omega$) inside the Arduino chip between `Pin 2` and `+5V`, and you wire the button between **`Pin 2` and `GND`**. That creates an **active-low** input:
    - **Button released (open):** Internal pull-up gently holds `Pin 2` at **`HIGH` (`1`, $5.0\text{ V}$)**.
    - **Button pressed (closed to `GND`):** The button connects `Pin 2` directly to `GND`, so `digitalRead(2)` reads **`LOW` (`0`, $0.0\text{ V}$)**.

### Bridge Notes
Arduino Uno sketches are written in **C/C++**, which uses the exact same variables, functions, and `if/else` decisions you learned in Track P with three small syntax rules:
1. **Curly braces `{ ... }` instead of indentation** mark the body of every function (`void setup() { ... }`, `void loop() { ... }`) and `if/else` block.
2. **Semicolons `;`** terminate every statement line (forgetting a `;` at the end of a line is the most common C/C++ compiler error).
3. **Explicit variable types (`int`, `float`, `bool`)** are written in front of a variable name the first time you declare it (for example, `int pin_reading = digitalRead(2);`).

When wiring a pushbutton to a microcontroller input pin, **never leave the pin unconnected ("floating") when the switch is open**. Calling `pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP);` inside `setup()` connects an internal $20\text{–}50\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor inside the ATmega328P chip between `Pin 2` and `+5.0 V`, while you wire the external pushbutton between `Pin 2` and `GND`. That creates a rock-solid **active-low** safety input—reading `HIGH` (`1`) when released and `LOW` (`0`) when pressed:

| Button State (`Pin 2` wired to `GND`, `INPUT_PULLUP`) | Voltage on `Pin 2` | `digitalRead(2)` Return Value | Safety LED Action (`Pin 13`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Released (Open switch)** | $5.0\text{ V}$ (via internal pull-up) | `HIGH` (`1`) | Normal operation (`LOW` / off) |
| **Pressed (Closed to `GND`)** | $0.0\text{ V}$ (connected to `GND`) | `LOW` (`0`) | Active! Turn LED on (`HIGH`) |

### Starter Code (runs as-is in Wokwi — fill in the TODOs)
Complete `check_active_low_button` (`TODO 1`) and `setup()` (`TODO 2`) in your Wokwi Arduino Uno sketch so it self-tests both button states (`HIGH` = released, `LOW` = pressed to `GND`) at startup and prints the exact expected verification lines to the Serial Monitor, then runs live in `loop()`:

```cpp
int check_active_low_button(int pin_reading) {
  // TODO 1: With INPUT_PULLUP and button wired to GND, pressing the button makes pin_reading == LOW.
  //         If pin_reading == LOW, return HIGH (turn LED on); otherwise return LOW (turn LED off).
  return LOW;
}

void print_test_case(const char* label, int pin_reading) {
  int led_out = check_active_low_button(pin_reading);
  Serial.print(label);
  Serial.print(" | pin_reading=");
  Serial.print(pin_reading == HIGH ? "HIGH(1)" : "LOW(0)");
  Serial.print(" -> led_out=");
  Serial.println(led_out == HIGH ? "HIGH(1)" : "LOW(0)");
}

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(9600);
  // TODO 2: Configure Pin 2 with INPUT_PULLUP and Pin 13 as OUTPUT
  pinMode(2, INPUT);
  pinMode(13, OUTPUT);

  // Automated self-test printed to Serial Monitor at startup:
  print_test_case("Released", HIGH);
  print_test_case("Pressed ", LOW);
}

void loop() {
  int pin_reading = digitalRead(2);
  int led_out = check_active_low_button(pin_reading);
  digitalWrite(13, led_out);
  delay(50);
}
```

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Using `pinMode(2, INPUT)` instead of `pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP)` without an external resistor:** The input pin floats and triggers randomly when your hand gets near the wire. Always use `INPUT_PULLUP` for simple pushbuttons wired to `GND`.
- **Wiring the pushbutton across two pins on the same always-connected side of a 4-leg tactile switch:** In Wokwi (`wokwi-pushbutton`), pins `1.l` and `1.r` are permanently connected together internally, and `2.l` and `2.r` are permanently connected together. Always wire `Pin 2` to **`1.l`** and `GND` to **`2.l`** (or diagonally across the button) so the switch is open until pressed.

## Check yourself

1. **Active-low logic:** When `Pin 2` is configured with `pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP);` and a button is wired between `Pin 2` and `GND`, what does `digitalRead(2)` return when your finger is **off** the button, and what does it return when you **press** the button?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - Finger **off** (switch open): **`HIGH` (`1`)**, because the internal pull-up resistor holds the pin at $5.0\text{ V}$.
   - Button **pressed** (switch closed to `GND`): **`LOW` (`0`)**, because the switch connects the pin directly to $0.0\text{ V}$ (`GND`).
   </details>

2. **Pull-up current when pressed:** If the Arduino Uno's internal pull-up resistor is $25\text{ k}\Omega$ ($25{,}000\ \Omega$) connected to $5.0\text{ V}$, how much current flows through the button to `GND` while it is held down?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   By Ohm's Law, $I = \frac{5.0\text{ V}}{25{,}000\ \Omega} = 0.0002\text{ A} = \mathbf{0.2\text{ mA}}$—tiny and completely safe!
   </details>

3. **Driving heavy loads:** Can you connect a brushed DC motor that draws $500\text{ mA}$ directly between Arduino `Pin 13` (`OUTPUT`) and `GND`?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   **Never!** An Arduino Uno GPIO pin can safely supply at most **$20\text{ mA}$ continuous** ($40\text{ mA}$ absolute maximum). Drawing $500\text{ mA}$ will permanently burn out the output transistor inside the microcontroller chip; motors require a MOSFET or H-bridge driver (Track 01).
   </details>

## Recap
- An Arduino Uno (`5.0 V`) sketch runs `setup()` once and `loop()` forever, driving `OUTPUT` pins to `HIGH` ($5.0\text{ V}$, $\le 20\text{ mA}$) or `LOW` ($0.0\text{ V}$).
- Configuring a button pin with **`pinMode(pin, INPUT_PULLUP)`** and wiring the switch to `GND` eliminates floating-pin noise and produces **active-low** readings (`HIGH` when released, `LOW` when pressed).
