### Idea 1: Effort (pressure or electrical push) and Flow (water stream or charge movement)
- **Primary resource:** [HyperPhysics — Water Analogy to DC Circuits](http://hyperphysics.phy-astr.gsu.edu/hbase/electric/watcir.html) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Water Analogy to DC Circuits`** comparing a water pump + return reservoir to an electrical source + return path.
  - **What to skip:** Skip sub-links to semiconductor transistor junctions.
  - **Key takeaway:** You cannot see electrons moving inside a copper wire, so engineers picture a closed **water pipe loop**. A **pump** creates a pressure difference (**Effort**, measured in Volts `V` in electricity) between its outlet and its low-pressure return tank ($0\text{ V}$ reference). That push drives a steady stream of water (**Flow**, measured in Amperes `A` in electricity) out of the pump, through the pipe, and all the way back to the return tank. If the loop is broken (a valve is shut or a wire is disconnected), **Effort** can still exist at the pump outlet, but **Flow** drops to zero!
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun Tutorials — What is a Circuit? ("Closed Loops")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/what-is-a-circuit) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Circuit Basics`** and **`Short and Open Circuits`**.

### Idea 2: Power as Effort times Flow and constriction as opposition to flow
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Hydraulic analogy ("Principal equivalents")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_analogy) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Equation examples`** and **`Principal equivalents`** (pressure difference $\leftrightarrow$ electrical push, flow rate $\leftrightarrow$ electrical flow, pipe constriction $\leftrightarrow$ electrical opposition).
  - **What to skip:** Skip acoustic wave impedance equations.
  - **Key takeaway:** A narrow **constriction** in a pipe opposes water movement: for the same pump push (**Effort**), doubling the constriction cuts the **Flow** in half ($\text{Flow} = \frac{\text{Effort}}{\text{Opposition}}$). Even better, across hydraulics, mechanics, and electricity, the rate of work (**Power**, in **Watts `W`**) is always the product of **Effort and Flow**:

$$
P_{\text{watts}} = \text{Effort} \times \text{Flow}
$$

### Bridge Notes
Before you wire a single component in the Falstad simulator in Lesson `S0.1`, this **water-pipe mental model** gives you physical intuition for every electrical quantity you will meet in Stage 0 and revisit in Track 00 (`Lesson 00.1`):

| Water Loop Quantity | What It Does Physically | Electrical Twin (Stage 0) | Mechanical Twin (`Track P` & `00.1`) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Return Tank (`0 Pa`)** | Zero-pressure baseline tank where all water returns | $0\text{ V}$ reference point | Floor / zero-height reference |
| **Pump Push (`Effort`)** | Pressure difference pushing water through the loop | Electrical push (`V`, Volts) | Force (`N`) or Torque ($\tau$, `N·m`) |
| **Stream Rate (`Flow`)** | Volume of water passing a point every second | Charge flow rate (`A`, Amperes) | Speed (`m/s`) or Angular speed (`rad/s`) |
| **Pipe Constriction** | Narrow sponge/nozzle that opposes flow | Opposition (`Ω`, Ohms) | Sliding surface friction |
| **Work Rate (`Effort × Flow`)** | How fast the stream can spin a waterwheel | Power $P = V \cdot I$ (`W`, Watts) | Power $P = F \cdot v = \tau \cdot \omega$ (`W`) |

Notice the two universal rules of **Effort and Flow** that govern every water loop, battery circuit, and robot joint:
1. **Flow requires both a push (`Effort`) and a complete closed loop:** A pump creates a pressure difference (`Effort`) between its high-pressure outlet and its zero-pressure return tank, driving water (`Flow`) through the pipe constriction (`Opposition`). Doubling the pump push (`Effort`) doubles the stream (`Flow`), whereas doubling the pipe constriction cuts `Flow` in half ($\text{Flow} = \text{Effort} / \text{Opposition}$). If a valve is shut so the loop is broken, `Effort` still sits waiting at the pump outlet, but `Flow` immediately drops to zero.
2. **Power (`Watts`) is always `Effort * Flow`:** The rate of useful work delivered (or heat dissipated in a narrow pipe constriction) is always the product of **Effort** and **Flow**. A high-pressure pump with a closed valve (`Effort = 9.0`, `Flow = 0.0`) delivers $9.0 \times 0.0 = 0.0\text{ W}$ of power, just as an unplugged battery delivers $0.0\text{ W}$ until a closed circuit allows charge to flow.

def compute_flow(effort_v, opposition_ohm):

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Thinking "Effort flows through a wire":** Water pressure (**Effort**) does not flow; pressure *pushes* across two points, while water (**Flow**) moves through the pipe. In the same way, Volts (`V`) are measured *across* two points, while Amperes (`A`) flow *through* a wire.
- **Expecting power when the loop is open:** Even a high-pressure $12.0\text{ V}$ supply delivers $0\text{ W}$ of power when the switch is open because $\text{Flow} = 0$, so $\text{Effort} \times 0 = 0\text{ W}$.
- **Confusing a short with an open valve:** zero opposition is a shorted path, not an open valve. This model cannot represent the flow a real supply would try to push through it, so `compute_flow` raises `ValueError` instead of returning `0.0`; an open valve is infinite opposition, which the model also cannot represent numerically.
