### Idea 1: PWM Duty Cycle ($0\%$ to $100\%$) and Average Voltage ($V_{\text{avg}} = \text{Duty} \times 5.0\text{ V}$)
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Pulse Width Modulation](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/pulse-width-modulation) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`What is Pulse-width Modulation?`**, **`Duty Cycle`**, and **`Examples`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip low-level timer register prescaler tables at the bottom.
  - **Key takeaway:** An Arduino Uno has no true analog voltage output (`DAC`); its pins can only output $0.0\text{ V}$ (`LOW`) or $5.0\text{ V}$ (`HIGH`). **Pulse-Width Modulation (`PWM`)** fakes an intermediate voltage by switching the pin between $5.0\text{ V}$ and $0.0\text{ V}$ hundreds of times per second: the percentage of each cycle Period $T$ that the pin stays `HIGH` is called the **duty cycle** ($\text{Duty} = \frac{T_{\text{on}}}{T} \times 100\%$), producing an **average voltage** $V_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\text{Duty}}{100\%} \times 5.0\text{ V}$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Falstad Circuit Simulator — Live Oscilloscope Square Wave](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/circuitjs.html) `[HTTP 200 — SPA/App: verify in-app]` — Open `Circuits` $\rightarrow$ `555 Timer Chip` $\rightarrow$ `Pulse Width Modulator` (or right-click any Square Wave voltage source $\rightarrow$ `View in Scope`) `[verify in-app]` to watch the live green `0 V / 5 V` square wave widen and narrow in the browser oscilloscope!

### Idea 2: 8-Bit Hardware PWM (`analogWrite` `0–255`) and PWM Frequency Limits
- **Primary resource:** [Wokwi Docs — `wokwi-led` ("Gamma correction" & "FPS")](https://docs.wokwi.com/parts/wokwi-led) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` & [Wokwi Docs — `wokwi-arduino-uno`](https://docs.wokwi.com/parts/wokwi-arduino-uno) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read **`Gamma correction`** and **`FPS`** on the `wokwi-led` page to see how Wokwi averages fast `analogWrite()` pulses into smooth visual LED brightness.
  - **What to skip:** Skip Charlieplexing and LED matrix multiplexing.
  - **Key takeaway:** Despite its confusing name, Arduino's `analogWrite(pin, value)` does **not** output a steady analog voltage—it outputs an **8-bit PWM square wave** where `value` ranges from **`0`** ($0\%$ duty cycle, always $0.0\text{ V}$) to **`255`** ($100\%$ duty cycle, always $5.0\text{ V}$), with `127` or `128` giving $\approx 50\%$ duty cycle ($2.50\text{ V}$ average). On the Arduino Uno, hardware PWM only works on the 6 pins marked with a tilde **`~`** on the board silkscreen: **Pins `3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11`**.
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun — Pulse Width Modulation ("Examples")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/pulse-width-modulation) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Review the LED dimming example under **`Examples`**.
  - **Optional desktop extra (not required):** [Wokwi Logic Analyzer Guide](https://docs.wokwi.com/guides/logic-analyzer) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Note that Wokwi's `wokwi-logic-analyzer` part does **not** show waveforms live in the browser; it downloads a `.vcd` file when you stop the simulation to be opened in the desktop app PulseView or GTKWave. Use **Falstad's built-in scope** for zero-install live waveforms in the browser!

### Bridge Notes
Look closely at the **resolution mismatch** between input and output on the Arduino Uno:
- **Analog Input (`analogRead(A0)`):** **10-bit** integer from **`0` to `1023`**.
- **PWM Output (`analogWrite(9, val)`):** **8-bit** integer from **`0` to `255`**.

For any 8-bit PWM command $\text{pwm}\_\text{val} \in [0, 255]$, the **duty cycle** fraction and **average voltage** $V_{\text{avg}}$ (at $V_{\text{cc}} = 5.0\text{ V}$) are:

$$
\text{Duty} = \frac{\text{pwm}\_\text{val}}{255}, \qquad V_{\text{avg}} = \left(\frac{\text{pwm}\_\text{val}}{255}\right) \times 5.0\text{ V}
$$

If an entire PWM cycle has period $T$ (where $T = \frac{1}{f}$), the pin stays at $5.0\text{ V}$ for **pulse width** $T_{\text{on}} = \text{Duty} \times T$ and stays at $0.0\text{ V}$ for $T_{\text{off}} = T - T_{\text{on}}$.

> **Looking Ahead — Arduino Uno `490 Hz` LED PWM vs. `20 kHz` Motor PWM:**
> On the Arduino Uno, `analogWrite()` switches at **`~490 Hz`** on pins `3, 9, 10, 11` ($T \approx 2.04\text{ ms}$) and **`~980 Hz`** on pins `5, 6` ($T \approx 1.02\text{ ms}$). Because human eyes cannot see flicker faster than $\sim 60\text{ Hz}$, `490 Hz` looks completely smooth on an LED! However, human ears hear vibrations between $20\text{ Hz}$ and $20{,}000\text{ Hz}$ (`20 kHz`). When we drive real motors in later tracks, `490 Hz` makes the motor coils sing with an annoying $490\text{ Hz}$ audible whine and causes high current ripple—so real motor drives configure hardware timers for **`20 kHz` to `25 kHz`** (ultrasonic) PWM!

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Calling `analogWrite(4, 128)` on a non-PWM pin (like Pin `2, 4, 7, 8, 12, 13`):** On the Arduino Uno, only pins **`3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11`** (marked with `~`) have hardware PWM timers! Calling `analogWrite` on a non-PWM pin like Pin `4` or `13` simply outputs `LOW` ($0\text{ V}$) for values `< 128` and steady `HIGH` ($5\text{ V}$) for values `>= 128`—it will not dim!
- **Passing a 10-bit `analogRead` value (`0..1023`) directly into 8-bit `analogWrite(9, val)` (`0..255`):** In Arduino C/C++, `analogWrite` only keeps the lowest 8 bits (`val % 256`), so as you turn a potentiometer from `0` to `1023`, the LED brightness wraps around and resets to dark **4 times** (`255 -> 0` at `256, 512, 768`)! Always divide the 10-bit reading by `4` (`pwm_val = adc_raw / 4`) to scale `0..1023` smoothly into `0..255`.

## Check yourself

1. **Instantaneous voltage vs. average voltage:** When you execute `analogWrite(9, 128)` on a $5.0\text{ V}$ Arduino Uno, if you freeze time at any single microsecond, can the voltage on Pin 9 ever be $2.50\text{ V}$?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   **No!** At any instant in time, Pin 9 is either strictly **`5.0 V` (`HIGH`)** or strictly **`0.0 V` (`LOW`)**. It is $2.51\text{ V}$ only on **average** because it spends roughly half of every $2.04\text{ ms}$ cycle at $5.0\text{ V}$ and the other half at $0.0\text{ V}$.
   </details>

2. **Scaling 10-bit ADC to 8-bit PWM:** Why does dividing `adc_raw` (`0..1023`) by `4` (`int pwm_val = adc_raw / 4;`) map the full potentiometer turn into `0..255`?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   Because $1024 \div 4 = 256$ (and in integer division, `1023 / 4 = 255`), dividing a 10-bit number (`0..1023`) by `4` maps it linearly into an 8-bit number (`0..255`).
   </details>

3. **490 Hz vs. 20 kHz PWM:** Why is the Arduino Uno's default `~490 Hz` / `~980 Hz` `analogWrite()` frequency fine for dimming an LED in Stage 0, whereas motor controllers in later tracks configure PWM timers for `20 kHz`?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   Human eyes blur flashes faster than $\sim 60\text{ Hz}$ into steady light, so `490 Hz` looks completely flicker-free on an LED. However, `490 Hz` and `980 Hz` sit right in the middle of human hearing ($20\text{ Hz}\text{–}20\text{ kHz}$), causing motor coils to vibrate like a speaker with a loud audible tone (plus higher current ripple). Switching at `20 kHz` moves the frequency above human hearing and smooths motor current.
   </details>

## Recap
- **PWM (Pulse-Width Modulation)** switches a digital pin between $5.0\text{ V}$ and $0.0\text{ V}$ at fixed frequency $f = 1/T$, producing an average voltage $V_{\text{avg}} = \text{Duty} \times 5.0\text{ V}$.
- On the Arduino Uno, **`analogWrite(pin, val)`** accepts an **8-bit integer (`0..255`)** on `~` pins (`3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 11`).
