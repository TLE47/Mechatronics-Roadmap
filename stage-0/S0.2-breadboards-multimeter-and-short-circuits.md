### Bridge Notes
A **schematic** shows *logical* connections between components regardless of physical position, whereas a **breadboard** is the physical plastic grid where you plug in wires. On a standard breadboard, each numbered row (`Row 1`, `Row 2`, etc.) has 5 connected holes (`a-e`) on the left of the center gap and 5 connected holes (`f-j`) on the right. A **node** is any continuous metal path shared by two or more component pins.

If a wire accidentally connects `5V` directly to `GND` with near-zero resistance ($R \approx 0.05\ \Omega$), Ohm's Law ($I = \frac{V}{R}$) predicts a massive current surge called a **short circuit** (whereas a broken wire with $R = \infty$ and zero current is an **open circuit**). **Important simulator note:** In Wokwi (`wokwi.com`), breadboard wires only route digital logic signals—if you short `5V` to `GND` or short both legs of an LED into `Row 10 (a-e)` in Wokwi, Wokwi does **not** display smoke, heat, or an overcurrent error! In [Falstad](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/circuitjs.html), placing a wire across a voltage source or across a resistor shows the current spiking to tens or hundreds of amps (or halting with a `"Matrix error"`). Always check for accidental row shorts using **continuity mode** (which beeps when resistance between two probes is near $0\ \Omega$) before turning power on.

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Plugging both legs of a resistor or button into the same 5-hole row (`a-e`):** Because holes `10a, 10b, 10c, 10d, 10e` are all clipped to the same metal strip underneath, plugging a resistor into `10a` and `10d` shorts its two legs together! Always plug one leg into `Row 10` and the other into a different row (like `Row 14`) or across the center ravine (`10e` to `10f`).
- **Assuming the center ravine is connected:** Holes `10a–10e` on the left are completely isolated from holes `10f–10j` on the right.
- **Probing voltage with multimeter leads left in the current (`A` / `mA`) socket:** Inside the current socket of a real multimeter is a near-zero-ohm wire ($R \approx 0.01\ \Omega$). Touching those probes across a `5V` or `12V` battery creates an immediate short circuit and blows the multimeter's internal fuse! Keep your red probe in the **`V / Ω`** socket at all times unless actively measuring series current.

## Check yourself

1. **Breadboard row tracing:** You plug the anode (`+`) of an LED into hole **`12b`** and the cathode (`-`) into hole **`12e`** on a breadboard. Will the LED ever light up? Why or why not?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   **No!** Holes `12a, 12b, 12c, 12d, 12e` are all connected to the exact same metal spring clip underneath the plastic. Any current reaching row `12` flows straight through that metal clip (`0 Ω`) instead of going through the LED. Move the cathode to a different row (such as `13b`) or across the center ravine (`12f`).
   </details>

2. **Voltmeter vs. Ammeter connection:** Which meter mode (Voltmeter or Ammeter) is connected **in parallel** (probing across a component without breaking the circuit), and which requires **breaking the loop** to wire it **in series**?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - **Voltmeter** is connected **in parallel** (across the two points whose voltage difference you want to read).
   - **Ammeter** is connected **in series** (you must open the circuit so the current flows *through* the meter).
   </details>

3. **Why Wokwi vs. Falstad for shorts:** If you accidentally wire an Arduino Uno's `5V` pin directly to `GND` on a Wokwi breadboard, what does Wokwi show, and why did we use Falstad to see the effect of a short circuit?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   Wokwi's breadboard only routes digital logic states and shows **nothing** (no error or overcurrent warning) when `5V` is shorted to `GND`. Falstad solves the analog Ohm's Law equations at every node, so a short circuit immediately shows massive current (`100.0 A` through a `0.05 Ω` wire) or flags a circuit error.
   </details>

## Recap
- On a breadboard, outer **power rails** run vertically, while inner **terminal rows (`a–e` and `f–j`)** connect in horizontal groups of 5 holes separated by the center ravine.
- Measure **voltage in parallel** (across a part) and **current in series** (in line with the loop), and always verify there is no continuity (`0 Ω` short) between `5V` and `GND` before powering up.
