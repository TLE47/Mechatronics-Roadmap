## Goal
Replace sudden step commands with a smooth **rest-to-rest cubic polynomial trajectory** ($q_d(t) = q_0 + (3s^2 - 2s^3)(q_f - q_0)$, $s = t/T$) whose velocity starts and ends at zero, and combine a soft PD spring-damper with **gravity feedforward** ($\tau_{\text{ff}} = m g L \cos q$) for safe, human-friendly **joint-space compliant control**.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Rest-to-rest cubic polynomial trajectories ($q_d(s) = q_0 + (3s^2 - 2s^3)\Delta q$) with zero endpoint velocity**: How scaling normalized time $s = t/T \in [0, 1]$ by $3s^2 - 2s^3$ starts and stops the joint smoothly with $\dot{q}_d(0) = \dot{q}_d(T) = 0$ and peak midpoint speed $\dot{q}_{\max} = 1.5 \frac{\Delta q}{T}$.
  2. **Joint-space compliant PD control with gravity feedforward ($\tau = K_p e - K_d \dot{q} + m g L \cos q$)**: How predicting gravity torque $\tau_g(q) = m g L \cos q$ lets a robot arm hold poses with zero steady-state sag even at low, springy $K_p$ and $K_i = 0$.
- **New syntax:** None (uses Python functions, `math.cos`, and `for` loops).

## Learn

### Idea 1: Rest-to-rest cubic polynomial trajectories ($q_d(s) = q_0 + (3s^2 - 2s^3)\Delta q$) with zero endpoint velocity
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Cubic Hermite spline ("Unit interval [0, 1]")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_Hermite_spline) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Cubic Hermite spline`** and **`Unit interval [0, 1]`** (notice the basis function $h_{01}(t) = 3t^2 - 2t^3$).
  - **What to skip:** Skip cardinal and Catmull-Rom computer-graphics tangents.
  - **Key takeaway:** Commanding a sudden step jump (`q_des = 1.0 rad` at $t = 0$) asks a heavy robot arm to teleport instantaneously, slamming the motor into current saturation and rattling the gears! Instead, for a move of $\Delta q = q_f - q_0$ over duration $T$, define normalized time $s = \operatorname{clip}(t / T,\ 0.0,\ 1.0)$. The **cubic polynomial trajectory** $q_d(t) = q_0 + (3s^2 - 2s^3)\Delta q$ and its velocity $\dot{q}_d(t) = \frac{6s(1-s)}{T}\Delta q$ guarantee $\dot{q}_d(0) = 0$, $\dot{q}_d(T) = 0$, and a smooth bell-shaped speed profile peaking at $s = 0.5$ with $\dot{q}_{\max} = 1.5 \frac{\Delta q}{T}$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Modern Robotics (Lynch & Park) — Trajectory Generation](https://hades.mech.northwestern.edu/index.php/Modern_Robotics) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Chapter 9 covers point-to-point cubic time scaling.

### Idea 2: Joint-space compliant PD control with gravity feedforward ($\tau = K_p e - K_d \dot{q} + m g L \cos q$)
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Feed forward (control) ("Overview" & "Benefits")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feed_forward_%28control%29) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Feed forward (control)`**, **`Overview`**, and **`Benefits`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip gene-regulatory biological networks.
  - **Key takeaway:** In `Lesson 05.1`, you saw that a pure PD controller sags under gravity unless you either crank $K_p$ very high (making the arm stiff and dangerous around humans) or turn on $K_i$ (which winds up if a human pushes the arm!). Because we already know the exact lever torque of gravity from `Lesson P.12` ($\tau_g(q) = m g L_{\text{com}} \cos q$, with $q$ measured from horizontal), we can add **gravity feedforward** directly to a soft PD controller ($K_i = 0$): the arm floats weightlessly with **zero steady-state sag** while remaining gentle and compliant if bumped by hand!
  - **Alternative resource:** [Wikipedia — Impedance control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impedance_control) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Impedance control`** and **`Principle`**.

### Bridge Notes
Every collaborative robot arm combines two ingredients inside its $1\text{ kHz}$ control loop: a smooth **rest-to-rest cubic polynomial trajectory** ($s = \operatorname{clip}(t/T,\ 0,\ 1)$) and **joint-space compliant PD control with gravity feedforward**:

$$
q_d(t) = q_0 + (3s^2 - 2s^3)(q_f - q_0), \qquad \dot{q}_d(t) = \frac{6s(1 - s)}{T}(q_f - q_0)
$$

$$
\tau_{\text{cmd}} = \underbrace{K_p\big(q_d(t) - q\big) + K_d\big(\dot{q}_d(t) - \dot{q}\big)}_{\text{Compliant Virtual Spring-Damper}} + \underbrace{m g L_{\text{com}} \cos(q)}_{\text{Gravity Feedforward } \tau_{\text{ff}}(q)}
$$

Why is **gravity feedforward** superior to an integral term $K_i$ for interactive robot arms that work around humans?
1. If you set $K_p = 0$ and keep only viscous damping plus **gravity feedforward** ($\tau_{\text{cmd}} = -K_d \dot{q} + m g L_{\text{com}}\cos q$), the arm enters **"Zero-Gravity Lead-Through Teaching Mode"**: a human operator can grab the gripper, guide the arm smoothly to any pose in space, and release it—and the link stays floating weightlessly wherever you let go!
2. Even when tracking a trajectory with a gentle, human-safe spring stiffness ($K_p = 2.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$), gravity feedforward cancels $100\%$ of the link weight at horizontal ($q = 0\text{ rad}$), achieving $0.000\text{ rad}$ steady-state sag without any integrator windup when pushed.

```python
import math


def cubic_trajectory(q0, qf, duration_T, t):
    s = max(0.0, min(1.0, t / duration_T))
    dq = qf - q0
    q_d = q0 + (3.0 * s**2 - 2.0 * s**3) * dq
    dq_d = (6.0 * s * (1.0 - s) / duration_T) * dq
    return q_d, dq_d


def compliant_gravity_command(q_des, dq_des, q, dq, kp, kd, mass_kg, l_com_m):
    tau_pd = kp * (q_des - q) + kd * (dq_des - dq)
    tau_ff = mass_kg * 9.81 * l_com_m * math.cos(q)
    return tau_pd + tau_ff


for t_s in [0.0, 0.5, 1.0]:
    pos, vel = cubic_trajectory(q0=0.0, qf=1.20, duration_T=1.0, t=t_s)
    print(f"Cubic t={t_s:.1f} s -> q_d={pos:.3f} rad | dq_d={vel:.3f} rad/s")

tau_hold = compliant_gravity_command(
    q_des=0.0, dq_des=0.0, q=0.0, dq=0.0, kp=2.0, kd=0.2, mass_kg=0.50, l_com_m=0.20
)
print(f"Compliant hold at horizontal q=0.0 rad (e=0): tau_cmd={tau_hold:.4f} N*m (exact gravity balance!)")
```

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Forgetting to clamp normalized time `s = max(0.0, min(1.0, t / T))` after $t > T$:** If $t$ keeps growing past $T$ ($s > 1$), $3s^2 - 2s^3$ turns negative and drives the robot arm backward to $-\infty$! Always clamp $s \in [0.0, 1.0]$ and set $\dot{q}_d = 0$ for $t \ge T$.
- **Using $\sin q$ instead of $\cos q$ when $q = 0$ is horizontal:** Always match your coordinate convention from `Lesson P.12` and `Lesson 02.1`: when $q = 0\text{ rad}$ is horizontal ($+x$-axis), gravity torque is maximum ($\cos 0 = 1$), and when $q = \pi/2\text{ rad}$ is straight up ($+y$-axis), gravity torque is zero ($\cos(\pi/2) = 0$).

## Recap
- A **rest-to-rest cubic polynomial trajectory** $q_d(t) = q_0 + (3s^2 - 2s^3)\Delta q$ ($s \in [0, 1]$) guarantees zero velocity at start and finish with peak midpoint velocity $1.5 \frac{\Delta q}{T}$.
- **Joint-space compliant PD control with gravity feedforward** ($\tau = K_p e + K_d \dot{e} + m g L \cos q$) holds poses without gravity droop while yielding safely like a mechanical spring.
