## Goal
Control a brushed DC motor's **direction (`Forward`, `Reverse`, `Coast`, `Brake`)** and **speed** using a 4-switch **H-Bridge** without triggering a destructive **shoot-through** short circuit, and calculate motor **back-EMF** ($V_{\text{emf}} = K_e \cdot \omega$) and locked-rotor **stall current** ($I_{\text{stall}} = V_s / R_m$).

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **H-Bridge 4-switch topology (`Forward`, `Reverse`, `Coast`, `Brake`) and shoot-through prevention**: Arranging four switches in an "H" around a motor so closing diagonal pairs reverses current direction, while never closing both top and bottom switches on the same side (`shoot-through`).
  2. **DC motor back-EMF ($V_{\text{emf}} = K_e \cdot \omega$) and locked-rotor stall current ($I_{\text{stall}} = V_s / R_m$)**: Why a spinning motor acts like a generator opposing the supply voltage, and why a stalled rotor ($\omega = 0$) draws maximum current limited only by winding resistance $R_m$.
- **New syntax:** Driving two direction pins (`IN1`, `IN2`) and one `analogWrite` PWM enable pin (`ENA`) on the Arduino Uno in Wokwi.
- **Target time (unmeasured — [log yours](../.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/beginner-timing-log.md)):** Typical: 1.5–2.0 hours | Slow-learner: 2.5–3.5 hours

## Learn

### Idea 1: H-Bridge 4-switch topology (Forward, Reverse, Coast, Brake) and shoot-through prevention
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — H-bridge ("General" & "Operation")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H-bridge) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`H-bridge`**, **`General`**, and **`DC motor driver`** (including the 4-switch `S1`–`S4` table and shoot-through warning).
  - **What to skip:** Skip relay and inverter construction sections.
  - **Key takeaway:** A single low-side MOSFET (`Lesson 01.1`) can only spin a motor in **one** direction. To spin both **Forward** and **Reverse**, an **H-Bridge** places four MOSFET switches (`S1, S2` on the left leg; `S3, S4` on the right leg) around the motor like the letter **H**:
    - **Forward (`IN1 = HIGH, IN2 = LOW`):** Top-left `S1` and bottom-right `S4` close; current flows **left-to-right** through the motor.
    - **Reverse (`IN1 = LOW, IN2 = HIGH`):** Top-right `S3` and bottom-left `S2` close; current flows **right-to-left** through the motor.
    - **Coast (`IN1 = LOW, IN2 = LOW`) vs. Brake (`IN1 = HIGH, IN2 = HIGH`):** Opening all switches lets the rotor coast freely; closing both bottom switches shorts the motor terminals together so its own generated current brakes it rapidly.
    - **Shoot-Through (`S1` and `S2` closed on the SAME leg):** Shorts $+V_{\text{supply}}$ straight to `GND` without going through the motor, destroying the MOSFETs! Integrated H-bridge driver chips include built-in logic that prevents shoot-through automatically—always match the chip's **Recommended Operating Supply Voltage (`VM`)** to your motor bus: use **`DRV8833`** only for $2.7\text{–}10.8\text{ V}$ motors ($11.8\text{ V}$ absolute max destruction limit—never connect `DRV8833` to a $12\text{ V}$ supply!), **`TB6612FNG`** for $4.5\text{–}13.5\text{ V}$ ($12\text{ V}$ motors), or **`DRV8871`** for $6.5\text{–}45\text{ V}$ ($12\text{–}24\text{ V}$ motors).
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun — Motors and Selecting the Right One](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/motors-and-selecting-the-right-one) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`What Makes A Motor Move?`** and **`DC Brush Motors - The Classic`**.

### Idea 2: DC motor back-EMF ($V_{\text{emf}} = K_e \cdot \omega$) and locked-rotor stall current ($I_{\text{stall}} = V_s / R_m$)
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Counter-electromotive force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-electromotive_force) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Counter-electromotive force`** and **`Details`** (electric motor back-EMF and starting current).
  - **What to skip:** Skip AC transformer derivations.
  - **Key takeaway:** Every spinning DC motor is simultaneously a **generator**! As the rotor spins at angular velocity $\omega$ (`rad/s`), its coils cut through the permanent magnets' field and generate a reverse voltage called **back-EMF** ($V_{\text{emf}} = K_e \cdot \omega$) that opposes the supply voltage $V_s$. Given winding resistance $R_m$, the motor current is $I_m = \frac{V_s - K_e \cdot \omega}{R_m}$:
    - **At stall ($\omega = 0$, startup or jammed shaft):** Back-EMF is zero ($V_{\text{emf}} = 0\text{ V}$), so the motor draws its maximum **stall current** $I_{\text{stall}} = \frac{V_s}{R_m}$ (often $5\times$ to $10\times$ higher than normal running current!).
    - **At fast no-load spin ($V_{\text{emf}} \approx V_s$):** Back-EMF nearly cancels $V_s$, so current drops close to zero.

### Bridge Notes
Inside a brushed DC motor with winding resistance $R_m$ (in $\Omega$) and back-EMF constant $K_e$ (in $\text{V}/(\text{rad/s})$), the steady-state motor current $I_m$ at supply voltage $V_s$ and shaft speed $\omega$ is governed by Ohm's Law across the remaining voltage difference $V_s - V_{\text{emf}}$:

$$
V_{\text{emf}} = K_e \cdot \omega, \qquad I_m = \frac{V_s - V_{\text{emf}}}{R_m}, \qquad I_{\text{stall}} = \frac{V_s}{R_m} \quad (\text{at }\omega = 0)
$$

For example, if a $12.0\text{ V}$ gearmotor has winding resistance $R_m = 4.0\ \Omega$ and $K_e = 0.10\text{ V}/(\text{rad/s})$:
- **Stalled ($\omega = 0\text{ rad/s}$):** $V_{\text{emf}} = 0.0\text{ V} \implies I_{\text{stall}} = \frac{12.0}{4.0} = \mathbf{3.00\text{ A}}$!
- **Spinning at $\omega = 100.0\text{ rad/s}$:** $V_{\text{emf}} = 0.10 \times 100.0 = 10.0\text{ V} \implies I_m = \frac{12.0 - 10.0}{4.0} = \mathbf{0.50\text{ A}}$.

On an Arduino Uno driving a $12\text{ V}$-rated H-bridge breakout (`TB6612FNG`, rated up to $13.5\text{ V}$ operating; or `DRV8833` if using a $6\text{–}9\text{ V}$ motor supply $\le 10.8\text{ V}$), two digital pins (`IN1` on `Pin 7`, `IN2` on `Pin 8`) set the direction state, while a PWM pin (`ENA` on `~Pin 9`) sets speed from `0` to `255`:

| Signed Speed Command (`-255..+255`) | `IN1` (`Pin 7`) | `IN2` (`Pin 8`) | `PWM` (`~Pin 9`) | H-Bridge State |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **`+192` (`> 0`)** | `HIGH` (`1`) | `LOW` (`0`) | `192` | **`FORWARD`** |
| **`-192` (`< 0`)** | `LOW` (`0`) | `HIGH` (`1`) | `192` | **`REVERSE`** |
| **`0` (`== 0`)** | `LOW` (`0`) | `LOW` (`0`) | `0` | **`COAST`** |

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Sizing a motor driver or power supply for the motor's "no-load current" ($0.2\text{ A}$) instead of its stall current ($3.0\text{ A}$):** Every single time a motor starts from rest ($\omega = 0$) or pushes against a heavy load, it briefly draws $I_{\text{stall}} = V_s / R_m$. Always choose an H-bridge rated above $I_{\text{stall}}$.
- **Passing a negative integer like `-192` directly into `analogWrite(9, signed_cmd)`:** `analogWrite` expects an unsigned 8-bit number `0..255`. Always use the sign of `signed_cmd` to set `IN1`/`IN2` and pass the positive magnitude `abs(signed_cmd)` to `analogWrite`.

## Check yourself

1. **Calculating stall current:** You measure a $6.0\text{ V}$ brushed DC gearmotor's terminal resistance with an unpowered ohmmeter (`Lesson S0.5`) and read $R_m = 2.0\ \Omega$. What current will this motor draw at the instant you apply $6.0\text{ V}$ while the shaft is still at rest ($\omega = 0$)?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   At $\omega = 0$, $V_{\text{emf}} = 0\text{ V}$, so $I_{\text{stall}} = \frac{6.0\text{ V}}{2.0\ \Omega} = \mathbf{3.0\text{ A}}$.
   </details>

2. **Shoot-through hazard:** In a 4-MOSFET H-bridge, what happens if a wiring bug turns ON the top-left switch (`S1`, connected to $+12\text{ V}$) and the bottom-left switch (`S2`, connected to `GND`) at the exact same time?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   It creates a **shoot-through short circuit** directly from $+12\text{ V}$ to `GND` through `S1` and `S2` (bypassing the motor completely), destroying the MOSFETs unless the power supply current limit catches it.
   </details>

## Recap
- An **H-Bridge** uses 4 switches in an "H" around a motor to drive current **Forward** (`IN1=1, IN2=0`), **Reverse** (`IN1=0, IN2=1`), **Coast** (`0, 0`), or **Brake** (`1, 1`), while preventing **shoot-through**.
- A spinning DC motor generates **back-EMF** $V_{\text{emf}} = K_e \cdot \omega$ opposing the supply voltage; at $\omega = 0$, it draws peak **stall current** $I_{\text{stall}} = V_s / R_m$.
