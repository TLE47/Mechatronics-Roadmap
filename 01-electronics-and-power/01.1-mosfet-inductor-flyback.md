### Idea 1: Low-Side N-Channel MOSFET Switching and Common Ground
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Transistors ("Introduction" & "Switch")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/transistors) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Introduction`** and **`Switch`** (focus on the concept of a small control signal switching a large load current on the low side between the load and `GND`).
  - **What to skip:** Skip Bipolar Junction Transistor (`BJT`) base-current amplification math ($\beta / h_{\text{FE}}$) and amplifier biasing Modes—modern motor drives use voltage-controlled **MOSFETs** instead of BJTs!
  - **Key takeaway:** Because an Arduino Uno pin can only supply $20\text{ mA}$ at $5.0\text{ V}$ while a small motor draws $500\text{ mA}$ to $5\text{ A}$ at $12.0\text{ V}$, we place a logic-level **N-Channel MOSFET** (a 3-pin electronic switch with **Gate `G`**, **Drain `D`**, and **Source `S`**) between the motor's negative lead and `GND`. When the Arduino sets `Gate = 0 V` relative to `Source`, the MOSFET is an open switch (`OFF`); when `Gate = 5.0 V`, the MOSFET closes with tiny **on-resistance** ($R_{\text{DS(on)}} \approx 0.02\ \Omega$), letting motor current flow from the $+12\text{ V}$ supply through the motor and MOSFET to the shared **common ground (`GND`)**.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Falstad Circuit Simulator — N-MOSFET Switch](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/circuitjs.html) `[HTTP 200 — SPA/App: verify in-app]` — Open `Circuits` $\rightarrow$ `MOSFETs` $\rightarrow$ `Switch` `[verify in-app]` and click the Gate input switch between `0 V` and `5 V` to watch the Drain-to-Source path open and close.

### Idea 2: Inductors (Motor Coils), Inductive Kickback ($V = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$), and Flyback Diode Clamping
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Diodes ("Flyback Diodes and Voltage Spike Suppression")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/diodes) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` & [SparkFun — Motors and Selecting the Right One](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/motors-and-selecting-the-right-one) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** On the Motors page, read exact headings **`What Makes A Motor Move?`**, **`Electromagnetism`**, **`Electromagnets`**, and **`DC Brush Motors - The Classic`**. On the Diodes page, read exact headings **`Ideal Diodes`**, **`Forward Voltage`**, and **`Flyback Diodes and Voltage Spike Suppression`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip **`Zener Diodes`**, **`Rectifiers`**, and **`Logic Gates`** on the Diodes page.
  - **Key takeaway:** Inside every motor is a coiled wire electromagnet called an **inductor** (measured in **Henries `H`** or **millihenries `mH`**), which stores energy in a magnetic field and fights any sudden change in **current** ($V_L = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$). If a MOSFET suddenly switches OFF a $1.0\text{ A}$ motor current in $1\ \mu\text{s}$ with nowhere for that current to go, the collapsing magnetic field generates a massive hundred-volt **inductive kickback** spike that punches straight through the MOSFET! Wiring a **flyback diode** in parallel across the motor coil (pointing from the MOSFET Drain **upward** to $+12\text{ V}$) stays off during normal running, but instantly turns on when the MOSFET opens—giving the coil current a safe loop to recirculate and clamping the Drain voltage to $V_{\text{supply}} + 0.7\text{ V}$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Wikipedia — Flyback Diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flyback_diode) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`Operation`** and **`Induction at the opening of a contact`**.

### Bridge Notes
Compare the two energy-storage components you now know:
- A **capacitor** ($C$, Lesson S0.4) stores energy in an electric field and **resists sudden changes in voltage** ($V$).
- An **inductor** ($L$, a coil of wire inside a motor or relay, measured in **Henries `H`** or **`mH`**, $10^{-3}\text{ H}$) stores energy in a magnetic field and **resists sudden changes in current** ($I$), obeying:

$$
V_L = L \cdot \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}
$$


To switch a $12.0\text{ V}$ motor coil with a $5.0\text{ V}$ Arduino pin, we use a **low-side N-Channel MOSFET**:
1. **Source (`S`)** connects to **common ground (`GND`)**—the negative terminal of the $12\text{ V}$ battery **must** be tied to the Arduino's `GND` so the $5.0\text{ V}$ Gate signal has a shared $0\text{ V}$ reference, plus a $10\text{ k}\Omega$ **gate pull-down resistor** from Gate to `GND` so the motor stays off while the Arduino boots.
2. **Drain (`D`)** connects to the bottom terminal of the motor coil (whose top terminal connects to $+12.0\text{ V}$).
3. **Flyback diode:** Connected directly across the motor coil with its **cathode (`-` stripe) at $+12.0\text{ V}$** and **anode (`+`) at the MOSFET Drain**. Without this diode, opening the MOSFET causes $\frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$ to spike Drain voltage to $V_{\text{supply}} + L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$ (hundreds of volts!). With the flyback diode, Drain voltage is safely clamped to $V_{\text{supply}} + V_f \approx 12.7\text{ V}$.

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Installing the flyback diode upside down (cathode stripe pointing down to Drain instead of up to $+12\text{ V}$):** During normal operation when the MOSFET turns ON, if the diode points downward from $+12\text{ V}$ to Drain (`0 V`), it conducts directly from $+12\text{ V}$ to `GND` around the motor—creating a dead short circuit that burns out both the diode and the MOSFET! The diode's **cathode stripe (`-`) MUST connect to $+V_{\text{supply}}$**, and its **anode (`+`) to the MOSFET Drain** (reverse-biased during normal operation).
- **Forgetting to connect Common Ground between the $12\text{ V}$ battery and the Arduino Uno:** If the $12\text{ V}$ battery's negative terminal is not connected to Arduino `GND`, the Arduino's $5\text{ V}$ Gate signal has no return path to the MOSFET's Source pin, so the MOSFET never turns on reliably (or floats half-on and overheats).
- **Using a non-logic-level MOSFET (like an older `IRF540N` instead of a logic-level `IRLZ44N`):** Standard MOSFETs need $10\text{ V}$ on the Gate to turn fully on; at $5.0\text{ V}$ from an Arduino Uno, they only open halfway with high resistance and overheat. Always check that the MOSFET datasheet specifies a low $R_{\text{DS(on)}}$ at **$V_{\text{GS}} = 4.5\text{ V}$ or $5.0\text{ V}$** ("Logic-Level Gate Drive").

## Check yourself

1. **Capacitor vs. Inductor duality:** A capacitor resists sudden changes in which electrical quantity ($V$ or $I$)? Which electrical quantity does an inductor (motor coil) resist changing suddenly?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - A **capacitor** resists sudden changes in **voltage ($V$)**.
   - An **inductor** resists sudden changes in **current ($I$)**.
   </details>

2. **Flyback diode orientation:** In a low-side N-Channel MOSFET motor switch (motor wired between $+12\text{ V}$ and MOSFET Drain), which leg of the flyback diode (`Anode` or `Cathode stripe`) connects to $+12\text{ V}$, and why doesn't current flow through the diode while the MOSFET is ON?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   The **Cathode (`-` stripe)** connects to **`+12 V`**, and the **Anode (`+`)** connects to the **MOSFET Drain**. While the MOSFET is ON (Drain $\approx 0\text{ V}$), the diode's cathode is at $+12\text{ V}$ and its anode is at $0\text{ V}$ (**reverse-biased**), so the diode blocks current completely until the MOSFET switches OFF and the inductor drives the Drain voltage above $+12.7\text{ V}$.
   </details>

3. **Why use a $10\text{ k}\Omega$ Gate-to-Source pull-down resistor?** During the first few milliseconds when an Arduino Uno powers up, all GPIO pins start as floating inputs (`INPUT`). What could happen to a MOSFET motor stage if there is no $10\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor between Gate and `GND`?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   A MOSFET Gate acts like a tiny capacitor: if connected to a floating pin, stray static charge can hold the Gate at $2\text{–}4\text{ V}$, causing the motor to jerk unexpectedly at power-on or leaving the MOSFET half-on where its high resistance causes it to overheat! A $10\text{ k}\Omega$ pull-down resistor bleeds any charge to `GND` so the MOSFET stays strictly OFF until `setup()` drives the pin.
   </details>

4. **Bridge to the H-Bridge (`Track 01` next):** A single low-side MOSFET + flyback diode can control a motor's **speed** from $0\%$ to $100\%$ using PWM, but can it reverse the motor's **direction**?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   **No!** With one MOSFET, current can only flow through the motor in one direction (from $+12\text{ V}$ down to `GND`). To spin a brushed DC motor both **forward and reverse**, we arrange **4 MOSFETs** (each with its own flyback diode) in the shape of the letter **H** around the motor—called an **H-Bridge**, which is the very next topic in Track 01!
   </details>

## Recap
- A logic-level **N-Channel MOSFET** (`Gate`, `Drain`, `Source`) switches heavy motor currents on the low side using a $5.0\text{ V}$ Arduino pin, provided the motor supply and Arduino share a **common ground (`GND`)**.
- Motor windings are **inductors** ($L$) that generate a destructive voltage spike ($V_L = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$) when current is turned off, requiring a parallel reverse-biased **flyback diode** (cathode stripe to $+V_{\text{supply}}$) to clamp the spike and recirculate coil current safely.
