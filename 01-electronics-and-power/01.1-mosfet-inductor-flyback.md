Use a logic-level **N-Channel MOSFET** (`Gate`, `Drain`, `Source`) so a $5.0\text{ V}$ Arduino Uno pin can safely switch a high-current $12.0\text{ V}$ motor load sharing a **common ground**, and place a parallel **flyback diode** across an **inductor** (motor coil) in Falstad to clamp destructive turn-off voltage spikes.

## Learn

### Idea 1: Low-side N-Channel MOSFET switching and common ground
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Transistors ("Introduction" & "Applications I: Switches")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/transistors) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Introduction`**, **`Applications I: Switches`**, and **`Transistor Switch`** (focus on a small control signal switching a large load current on the low side between the load and `GND`).
  - **What to skip:** Skip Bipolar Junction Transistor (`BJT`) base-current amplification math ($\beta / h_{\text{FE}}$) and amplifier biasing modes—modern motor drives use voltage-controlled **MOSFETs** instead of BJTs!
  - **Key takeaway:** Because an Arduino Uno pin can only supply $20\text{ mA}$ at $5.0\text{ V}$ while a small motor draws $500\text{ mA}$ to $5\text{ A}$ at $12.0\text{ V}$, we place a logic-level **N-Channel MOSFET** (a 3-pin electronic switch with **Gate `G`**, **Drain `D`**, and **Source `S`**) between the motor's negative lead and `GND`. When the Arduino sets `Gate = 0 V` relative to `Source`, the MOSFET is an open switch (`OFF`); when `Gate = 5.0 V`, the MOSFET closes with tiny **on-resistance** ($R_{\text{DS(on)}} \approx 0.02\ \Omega$), letting motor current flow from the $+12\text{ V}$ supply through the motor and MOSFET to the shared **common ground (`GND`)**.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Falstad Circuit Simulator — N-MOSFET Switch](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/circuitjs.html) `[verify in-app]` — Open `Circuits` $\rightarrow$ `MOSFETs` $\rightarrow$ `Switch` `[verify in-app]` and click the Gate input switch between `0 V` and `5 V` to watch the Drain-to-Source path open and close.

### Idea 2: Inductive kickback ($V_L = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$) and flyback diode clamping
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — Diodes ("Flyback Diodes and Voltage Spike Suppression")](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/diodes) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Ideal Diodes`**, **`Forward Voltage`**, and **`Flyback Diodes and Voltage Spike Suppression`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip **`Zener Diodes`**, **`Rectifiers`**, and **`Logic Gates`** on the Diodes page.
  - **Key takeaway:** Inside every motor is a coiled wire electromagnet called an **inductor** (measured in **Henries `H`** or **millihenries `mH`**), which stores energy in a magnetic field and fights any sudden change in **current** ($V_L = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$). If a MOSFET suddenly switches OFF a $2.0\text{ A}$ motor current in $2.0\ \mu\text{s}$ with nowhere for that current to go, the collapsing magnetic field generates a massive hundred-volt **inductive kickback** spike that punches straight through the MOSFET! Wiring a **flyback diode** in parallel across the motor coil (pointing from the MOSFET Drain **upward** to $+12\text{ V}$) stays off during normal running, but instantly turns on when the MOSFET opens—giving the coil current a safe loop to recirculate and clamping the Drain voltage to $V_{\text{supply}} + 0.7\text{ V} = 12.7\text{ V}$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun — Motors and Selecting the Right One](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/motors-and-selecting-the-right-one) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Read exact headings **`What Makes A Motor Move?`** and **`DC Brush Motors - The Classic`**.

### Bridge Notes
Compare the two energy-storage components you now know:
- A **capacitor** ($C$, Lesson S0.6) stores energy in an electric field and **resists sudden changes in voltage** ($V$).
- An **inductor** ($L$, a coil of wire inside a motor or relay, measured in **Henries `H`** or **`mH`**, $10^{-3}\text{ H}$) stores energy in a magnetic field and **resists sudden changes in current** ($I$), obeying:

$$
V_L = L \cdot \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}
$$

To switch a $12.0\text{ V}$ motor coil with a $5.0\text{ V}$ Arduino pin, we use a **low-side N-Channel MOSFET**:
1. **Source (`S`)** connects to **common ground (`GND`)**—the negative terminal of the $12\text{ V}$ battery **must** be tied to the Arduino's `GND` so the $5.0\text{ V}$ Gate signal has a shared $0\text{ V}$ reference, plus a $10\text{ k}\Omega$ **gate pull-down resistor** from Gate to `GND` so the motor stays off while the Arduino boots.
2. **Drain (`D`)** connects to the bottom terminal of the motor coil (whose top terminal connects to $+12.0\text{ V}$).
3. **Flyback diode:** Connected directly across the motor coil with its **cathode (`-` stripe) at $+12.0\text{ V}$** and **anode (`+`) at the MOSFET Drain**. Without this diode, opening a $2.0\text{ A}$ current through an $L = 0.5\text{ mH}$ ($0.0005\text{ H}$) coil in $\Delta t = 2.0\ \mu\text{s}$ ($2 \times 10^{-6}\text{ s}$) spikes Drain voltage to $12.0 + 0.0005 \times \frac{2.0}{0.000002} = 512.0\text{ V}$! With the flyback diode, Drain voltage is safely clamped to $12.0 + 0.7 = 12.7\text{ V}$.

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Installing the flyback diode upside down (cathode stripe pointing down to Drain instead of up to $+12\text{ V}$):** During normal operation when the MOSFET turns ON, if the diode points downward from $+12\text{ V}$ to Drain (`0 V`), it conducts directly from $+12\text{ V}$ to `GND` around the motor—creating a dead short circuit that burns out both the diode and the MOSFET! The diode's **cathode stripe (`-`) MUST connect to $+V_{\text{supply}}$**, and its **anode (`+`) to the MOSFET Drain** (reverse-biased during normal operation).
- **Forgetting to connect Common Ground between the $12\text{ V}$ battery and the Arduino Uno:** If the $12\text{ V}$ battery's negative terminal is not connected to Arduino `GND`, the Arduino's $5\text{ V}$ Gate signal has no return path to the MOSFET's Source pin, so the MOSFET never turns on reliably (or floats half-on and overheats).
- **Using a non-logic-level MOSFET (like an older `IRF540N` instead of a logic-level `IRLZ44N`):** Standard MOSFETs need $10\text{ V}$ on the Gate to turn fully on; at $5.0\text{ V}$ from an Arduino Uno, they only open halfway with high resistance and overheat. Always check that the MOSFET datasheet specifies a low $R_{\text{DS(on)}}$ at **$V_{\text{GS}} = 4.5\text{ V}$ or $5.0\text{ V}$** ("Logic-Level Gate Drive").

## Check yourself

1. **Capacitor vs. Inductor duality:** A capacitor resists sudden changes in which electrical quantity ($V$ or $I$)? Which electrical quantity does an inductor (motor coil) resist changing suddenly?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   - A **capacitor** resists sudden changes in **voltage ($V$)**.
   - An **inductor** resists sudden changes in **current ($I$)**.
   </details>

2. **Flyback diode orientation:** In a low-side N-Channel MOSFET motor switch (motor wired between $+12\text{ V}$ and MOSFET Drain), which leg of the flyback diode (`Anode` or `Cathode stripe`) connects to $+12\text{ V}$, and why doesn't current flow through the diode while the MOSFET is ON?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   The **Cathode (`-` stripe)** connects to **`+12 V`**, and the **Anode (`+`)** connects to the **MOSFET Drain**. While the MOSFET is ON (Drain $\approx 0\text{ V}$), the diode's cathode is at $+12\text{ V}$ and its anode is at $0\text{ V}$ (**reverse-biased**), so the diode blocks current completely until the MOSFET switches OFF and the inductor drives the Drain voltage above $+12.7\text{ V}$.
   </details>

3. **Why use a $10\text{ k}\Omega$ Gate-to-Source pull-down resistor?** During the first few milliseconds when an Arduino Uno powers up, all GPIO pins start as floating inputs (`INPUT`). What could happen to a MOSFET motor stage if there is no $10\text{ k}\Omega$ resistor between Gate and `GND`?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   The floating MOSFET **Gate** acts like a small capacitor that can pick up stray charge and turn the motor **ON unexpectedly** (or hold the MOSFET half-on where it overheats) before `setup()` runs. A $10\text{ k}\Omega$ pull-down resistor from Gate to `GND` holds the Gate safely at $0.0\text{ V}$ until the Arduino actively drives the pin `HIGH`.
   </details>

## Recap
- A **low-side N-Channel MOSFET** switches heavy $12\text{ V}$ loads on the negative (`GND`) side using a $5.0\text{ V}$ logic Gate signal referenced to a shared **common ground**.
- An **inductor** (motor coil, in Henries `H`) resists sudden changes in current ($V_L = L \frac{\Delta I}{\Delta t}$); a **flyback diode** wired across the coil (cathode to $+V_{\text{supply}}$, anode to Drain) safely clamps turn-off voltage spikes to $V_{\text{supply}} + 0.7\text{ V}$.
