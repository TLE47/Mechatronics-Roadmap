## Goal
Step a $12.0\text{ V}$ robot battery down to a clean $5.0\text{ V}$ logic rail by comparing the heat wasted in a **Linear Regulator (`LDO`)** ($P_{\text{heat}} = (V_{\text{in}} - V_{\text{out}}) \cdot I_{\text{load}}$) against a high-efficiency **Switching (`Buck`) Converter** ($I_{\text{in}} = \frac{V_{\text{out}} \cdot I_{\text{out}}}{\eta \cdot V_{\text{in}}}$), and build a complete **worst-case system power budget** in Python.

- **New ideas (max 2):**
  1. **Linear (`LDO`) regulator heat loss vs. switching (`Buck`) converter power conservation**: Why a linear regulator burns off excess voltage as heat like a variable resistor, whereas a buck converter chops and filters with an inductor and capacitor at $85\%\text{–}95\%$ efficiency.
  2. **System worst-case power budgeting and separate motor vs. logic voltage rails**: Summing peak currents across motor and logic rails so the battery fuse and regulators never brown out when motors stall.
- **New syntax:** None (Python arithmetic and functions from Track P).

## Learn

### Idea 1: Linear (LDO) regulator heat loss vs. switching (Buck) converter power conservation
- **Primary resource:** [Wikipedia — Buck converter ("Theory" & "Concept")](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_converter) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Read exact headings **`Buck converter`**, **`Theory`**, and **`Idealised case`**.
  - **What to skip:** Skip discontinuous-mode inductor ripple integrals.
  - **Key takeaway:** Robots typically carry a $12.0\text{ V}$ battery for motors, but the Arduino Uno and sensors need $5.0\text{ V}$. There are two ways to step $12.0\text{ V}$ down to $5.0\text{ V}$:
    - **Linear Regulator (`LDO`, like `7805` or `AMS1117`):** Acts like an automatic variable series resistor that drops the extra $12.0 - 5.0 = 7.0\text{ V}$. Input current equals output current ($I_{\text{in}} = I_{\text{load}}$), and all the dropped voltage turns straight into heat: $P_{\text{heat}} = (V_{\text{in}} - V_{\text{out}}) \cdot I_{\text{load}}$. Great for tiny currents ($\le 50\text{ mA}$), but at $0.50\text{ A}$ it burns $7.0 \times 0.50 = 3.50\text{ W}$ and cooks itself!
    - **Switching Step-Down (`Buck`) Converter:** Uses a fast PWM switch, an inductor, and a capacitor to convert high voltage at low current into low voltage at higher current with efficiency $\eta \approx 0.90$ ($90\%$): $P_{\text{in}} = \frac{P_{\text{out}}}{\eta} \implies I_{\text{in}} = \frac{V_{\text{out}} \cdot I_{\text{load}}}{\eta \cdot V_{\text{in}}}$.
  - **Alternative resource:** [Falstad Circuit Simulator — Buck Converter](https://www.falstad.com/circuit/circuitjs.html) `[verify in-app]` — Open `Circuits` $\rightarrow$ `Power Converters` $\rightarrow$ `Buck Converter` `[verify in-app]`.

### Idea 2: System worst-case power budgeting and separate motor vs. logic voltage rails
- **Primary resource:** [SparkFun — How to Power a Project](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/how-to-power-a-project) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]`
  - **What to read:** Review how portable projects budget battery voltage and current capacity across high-draw actuators and logic electronics.
  - **What to skip:** Skip solar panel charging circuits.
  - **Key takeaway:** Never power a motor from the Arduino Uno's onboard `5V` pin! Instead, split your robot into two power rails fed from the main $12.0\text{ V}$ battery:
    1. **Raw $12.0\text{ V}$ Motor Rail (`VMOT`):** Feeds the H-bridge motor drivers directly.
    2. **Regulated $5.0\text{ V}$ Logic Rail (`VCC`):** Stepped down from $12.0\text{ V}$ via a **Buck converter** to feed the Arduino and sensors cleanly.
    A **power budget** sums the worst-case (stall) currents on every rail so you can size your regulator and main battery fuse with a safe margin.
  - **Alternative resource:** [SparkFun — Electric Power](https://learn.sparkfun.com/tutorials/electric-power) `[HTTP 200 — Verified by scripts/check_links.py]` — Review exact headings **`What is Electric Power?`** and **`Calculating Power`**.

### Bridge Notes
Compare stepping $V_{\text{in}} = 12.0\text{ V}$ down to $V_{\text{out}} = 5.0\text{ V}$ for a $5.0\text{ V}$ logic and sensor rail drawing $I_{\text{load}} = 0.50\text{ A}$ ($P_{\text{out}} = 5.0 \times 0.50 = 2.50\text{ W}$). A **Linear Regulator (`LDO`)** passes the full load current straight from the $12.0\text{ V}$ battery ($I_{\text{in}} = I_{\text{load}} = 0.50\text{ A}$) and burns the entire $7.0\text{ V}$ voltage drop as heat ($3.50\text{ W}$). By contrast, a switching **Buck Converter** ($\eta = 90\%$) conserves power across its inductor-capacitor stage, drawing only $I_{\text{buck,in}} = 0.231\text{ A}$ from the $12.0\text{ V}$ battery and wasting just $0.28\text{ W}$:

$$
P_{\text{LDO,heat}} = (V_{\text{in}} - V_{\text{out}}) \cdot I_{\text{load}}, \qquad I_{\text{buck,in}} = \frac{V_{\text{out}} \cdot I_{\text{load}}}{\eta \cdot V_{\text{in}}}, \qquad P_{\text{buck,heat}} = V_{\text{in}} \cdot I_{\text{buck,in}} - V_{\text{out}} \cdot I_{\text{load}}
$$

| Regulator Type ($12.0\text{ V} \rightarrow 5.0\text{ V}$ at $I_{\text{load}} = 0.50\text{ A}$) | Current Drawn from $12\text{ V}$ Battery ($I_{\text{in}}$) | Input Power ($P_{\text{in}}$) | Wasted Heat ($P_{\text{heat}}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Linear Regulator (`LDO`)** | $0.500\text{ A}$ | $6.00\text{ W}$ | **$3.50\text{ W}$ (Overheats!)** |
| **Switching `Buck` Converter ($\eta = 90\%$)** | $0.231\text{ A}$ | $2.78\text{ W}$ | **$0.28\text{ W}$ (Runs cool!)** |

To complete your robot's worst-case **power budget**, keep the $12.0\text{ V}$ motor rail (`VMOT`) separate from the $5.0\text{ V}$ logic rail (`VCC`) and add the buck converter's $12\text{ V}$ input current ($I_{\text{buck,in}} = 0.231\text{ A}$) to the motor's $12\text{ V}$ stall current ($I_{\text{stall}} = 3.00\text{ A}$ from `Lesson 01.2`) to get total peak battery current $I_{\text{batt,max}} = 3.231\text{ A}$.

## Common mistakes & troubleshooting
- **Powering a hobby servo or DC motor from the Arduino Uno's onboard $5\text{ V}$ linear regulator (`VIN` fed by $12\text{ V}$):** When the servo stalls at $1.0\text{ A}$, the tiny onboard LDO has to dissipate $(12 - 5) \times 1.0 = 7.0\text{ W}$, causing immediate thermal shutdown and rebooting the Arduino! Always use an external Buck converter for $5\text{ V}$ servos and logic.
- **Assuming Buck converter input current equals output current:** Because a Buck converter conserves power ($V_{\text{in}} I_{\text{in}} \approx V_{\text{out}} I_{\text{out}} / \eta$), stepping $12\text{ V}$ down to $5\text{ V}$ draws **less** current from the $12\text{ V}$ battery than the $5\text{ V}$ load consumes!

2. **Why separate `VMOT` and `VCC` rails?** Why do we connect the H-bridge motor supply (`VMOT`) directly to the $12\text{ V}$ battery while feeding the Arduino `5V` rail through a separate Buck regulator?
   <details>
   <summary><strong>Answer</strong></summary>

   Separating the rails keeps multi-amp motor stall surges off the $5.0\text{ V}$ regulator so the microcontroller's logic voltage stays steady at $5.0\text{ V}$ even when the motor stalls.
   </details>

## Recap
- A **Linear Regulator (`LDO`)** wastes $P_{\text{heat}} = (V_{\text{in}} - V_{\text{out}}) \cdot I_{\text{load}}$ as heat, whereas a **Switching (`Buck`) Converter** steps voltage down at $\sim 90\%$ efficiency ($I_{\text{in}} = \frac{V_{\text{out}} I_{\text{load}}}{\eta V_{\text{in}}}$).
- A **system power budget** separates high-current motor power (`VMOT`) from regulated logic power (`VCC`) and sums worst-case stall currents to size fuses and supplies safely.
